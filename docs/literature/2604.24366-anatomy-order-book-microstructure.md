# The Anatomy of a Decentralized Prediction Market: Microstructure Evidence from the Polymarket Order Book

- **arXiv:** [2604.24366](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.24366). v1 27 Apr 2026; v2 14 May 2026 (Section 3 scope narrowed, SF2 redone as a per-level depth profile, SF8 reframed). q-fin.TR (also cs.GT, q-fin.GN). 16 pp, 9 figures, 5 tables.
- **Author:** Philipp D. Dubach (Zurich).
- **Code:** github.com/philippdubach/polymarket-microstructure (Zenodo 10.5281/zenodo.19811426). Code and panel parquets are released; the raw archive is available on request.
- **Read:** the full v2 text, plus v1 sections SF2 and SF8. The reviewer also inspected the on-chain join code (`polydata/onchain/join.py`, `trades.py`).

## Question

Microstructure sets the cost of holding an informational position, and prediction-market
microstructure is little studied. The paper is a cross-sectional, tick-level complement
to Tsang & Yang (2026, arXiv:2603.03136), which covers a single election market. It
makes two contributions:
- **(a)** feed-inferred trade direction is close to noise;
- **(b)** eight stylized facts.

## Data

**Order-book archive.** The public WebSocket market channel, 2026-02-21 → 04-15 (52 days).
- 30.3B rows, 623.8 GB, 385,198 market IDs; 3 hourly files are missing.
- Only `price_change` (99.2%) and `book_snapshot` (0.8%) are kept; `last_trade_price` is
  excluded.
- Two timestamps per row: exchange-received and archive-created.

**On-chain fills.** CTF Exchange V1 (0x4bFb41d5…982E) `OrderFilled`, 2026-02-28 → 03-27,
255.4M fills.
- Collected with `eth_getLogs`, deduplicated on (txHash, logIndex), skipping blocks less
  than 256 deep.
- Timestamps are linearly interpolated from one anchor block: maximum error 4 s, median
  2 s, checked on 50 blocks.

**Direction.** `takerAssetId = 0` means the taker paid USDC, i.e. bought (+1);
`makerAssetId = 0` gives −1.

**Token map.** CLOB REST `/markets/{conditionId}` resolved all markets. The author says
Gamma indexes only 34,764 markets; the reviewer suspects this is a query artifact.

**Panel** (rule committed, SHA-256 recorded): 600 markets.
- Top-100 by on-chain USDC volume ($4.56M to $96.0M).
- 500 random markets with ≥ 100 trades (seed 20260424).
- Keyword categories: Crypto 348, Sports 142, Other 75, Geopolitics 35.

## Methods

- **Spreads:** quoted half-spread in bps of mid, by mid decile.
- **Depth:** per-level share of top-10 depth, with KL divergence from uniform.
- **Block clock:** share of events within ±100 ms of a 2 s grid, with a binomial test.
- **Concentration:** volume-weighted maker HHI.
- **Latency:** archive-ingestion percentiles.
- **Wash:** a trade is flagged if maker = taker, or the same pair flips roles within 128
  blocks in the same market.
- **SF8 regression:** OLS of log depth at L10 on log seconds-to-close (HC3), with five
  specifications.
- **Glosten–Harris decomposition:** c = 60 s realized half-spread; φ = the residual.
- **Feed vs chain sign agreement:** LOOSE inference (every drop in resting size counts as
  a trade) over four disjoint 7-day windows, with a market-clustered bootstrap. STRICT
  inference is compared on six direction-dependent measures. Also: Wilson CIs, volume
  weighting, and a sampling-step sensitivity of 1, 10, 60 and 300 s.

## Findings

**Feed sign agreement** (top-100 markets).
- Mean 0.615 [0.579, 0.653], from 109 cells in 55 markets. Bucket-weighted 0.592
  [0.542, 0.659].
- Median 0.591, p10–p90 0.46–0.84.
- **STRICT vs chain, window A:** feed trade count is a median 0.23× the chain count. The
  effective spread flips sign in 16/24 markets (67%) and Kyle's λ in 15/25 (60%).
- **Window B:** spread flips in 15/30 (50%) and λ in 13/30 (43%). Both CIs contain 50%.

**SF1, spread by mid price.**

| Mid price | Median spread | Markets |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| < 0.10 | 1,818 bps | 33 |
| 0.1–0.2 | 1,339 bps | — |
| 0.3–0.4 | 2,581 bps | — |
| 0.4–0.6 | ~400 bps | 184 + 214 |
| > 0.9 | 53 bps | 4 |

The pattern is not monotone.

**SF2, depth.**
- Median L1 share 0.136, L2 0.103, L3 0.095, about 0.08 from L4 to L10.
- KL from uniform: median 0.087.
- 9% of markets have L1 > 0.5.

**SF3, block clock.** Median share near the grid is 0.102; the binomial test rejects in
351/600 markets.

**SF4, maker concentration.** Median HHI 0.031 (about 32 effective makers), p90 0.119,
maximum 0.40.

**SF5, effective half-spread by category.** Sports 0.0075, Geopolitics 0.0001,
Other −0.0004, **Crypto −0.0393**. These are implausible values.

**SF6, latency.** Median p50 of 41.5 ms and p99 of 6.1 s. This measures the collector,
not the exchange.

**SF7, wash share.** Median 0.97%, p90 4.5%, maximum 22.2%.

**SF8, depth vs time-to-close.**
- The slope falls across specifications: 0.818 → 0.550 (category fixed effects) → 0.305
  (+ volume) → **0.008** (full spec, t = 0.08, R² 0.57, n = 313).
- In the full spec: log duration +0.22, log p(1−p) −1.02, log volume +0.41.

**Glosten–Harris.** Medians are essentially 0; c ≈ effective spread and φ ≈ 0.

**Sampling-step sensitivity.** Roll is stable; effective spread and Amihud move 10–20%; λ
moves by orders of magnitude.

## Reusable conventions

- Direction from the USDC asset ID.
- Per-level depth share with KL divergence.
- Block-alignment share.
- Maker HHI, with 1/HHI as the number of effective makers.
- The two-tier wash rule.
- A panel committed by hash.
- Map tokens through CLOB REST rather than Gamma.
- Treat ingestion latency as a property of the collector.

## Stated limitations and future work

- Cannot separate an inventory premium from a behavioural premium (no maker inventory or
  time-on-book).
- Cross-section only, so no within-market depth decay.
- Wash is only a lower bound.
- The seller/buyer asymmetry may be MEV or retail; telling them apart needs mempool and
  relay data.
- `last_trade_price` is not evaluated.
- **Future:** V1 vs V2, and lead–lag with Kalshi, PredictIt and sportsbooks.

## Critical assessment

**Internal inconsistencies.**
- It says no fact needs the on-chain join, but SF4, SF5 and SF7 do.
- V2 cutover dates conflict: early April vs end of April. A companion paper cites
  2026-04-28.
- Half-spread vs full spread, and 200 vs 400 bps for the central decile.
- The text says matching uses 5 s buckets; the code matches on block, token and price.
- "Pre-registered", yet the SF8 full specification was added in v2. v1 converted a
  log-log slope of 0.305 to "about 6% per 10×", when roughly 50% is implied.

**Contaminated ground truth** (checked in the code).
- A CTF Exchange match emits one `OrderFilled` per maker plus a taker-summary record
  whose `taker` is the exchange contract. The repo does not filter it, so that record
  gets the opposite sign.
- Mint and merge matches get conventional directions.
- Ties are broken by row order (`sign.first()`).
- Interpolation error is comparable to the bucket size.
- Together these can pull agreement toward 50% and may produce the 60/40 skew. "Take
  direction from the chain" is right, but **59% is not a clean measure of feed
  accuracy.**

**Measurement artefacts.**
- SF1 in bps of mid is mostly mechanical: one tick at p = 0.05 is about 2,000 bps. A
  longshot premium needs tick-normalized spreads. The outer deciles hold only 4–33
  markets.
- Negative Crypto effective spreads and a degenerate Glosten–Harris split point to a
  stale mid or YES/NO price mismatches.
- The SF3 grid is not anchored to block times.
- The flip-pair wash rule can also flag ordinary round trips between a market maker and a
  frequent trader.

**Trust:**

| Sign-flip rates | low |

## Directions it opens

- **Canonical fill table:** one row per match (drop exchange-as-taker summaries or key on
  `OrdersMatched`); label each fill as transfer, mint or merge; aggressor = taker order;
  fold NO into YES. Cover the V1, NegRisk and V2 contracts.
- Re-run the feed-sign benchmark with exact block times, `last_trade_price` and the new
  `price_change` schema.
- **Tick-normalized spreads** around tick-size regimes, then re-test the longshot
  spread premium.
- **Event-time depth and spread paths to resolution** from live WebSocket capture.
- **The V1→V2 cutover as a natural experiment:** spreads, depth, maker set,
  fragmentation, fees.
- Maker HHI over time: persistence of maker identities and the effect of liquidity
  rewards.
- Graph-based wash detection: proxy wallets, funding, USDC transfers.
- Settlement lag (exchange timestamp → block) and feed staleness, as a latency product.
- Cross-venue lead–lag: Kalshi, and Binance for crypto Up/Down markets.
- A public market-day panel of corrected spread, impact and markout metrics.
