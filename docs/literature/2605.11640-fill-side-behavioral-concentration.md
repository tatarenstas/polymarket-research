# Fill-Side Behavioral Concentration on Polymarket: Identification Limits under Record-Level Attribution

- **arXiv:** [2605.11640](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.11640). q-fin.TR (also cs.CY, q-fin.CP).
  - **v1** (12 May 2026, 198 KB): "Fill-Side Non-Retail Trading on Polymarket: An Empirical Study of Behavioral Tiers and Microstructure Signatures Under Quote-Attribution Constraints".
  - **v2** (30 Jul 2026, 11 KB): retitled; a major correction with no new empirical run. The comment says 2 propositions and 2 tables, but the text has 3 of each.
- **Author:** Maksym Nechepurenko (ForesightFlow / Devnull FZCO, Dubai). It is the 4th paper in a series on event-linked perpetuals (2605.10400, .10428, .10486).
- **Artifacts:** github.com/ForesightFlow/event-linked-perps (`evaluation/paper4/`); Zenodo 10.5281/zenodo.20107449 and .20108387.
- **Read:** v2 in full; v1 §§1–5, the whale table, §9.8 and §10's tables closely; the rest of v1 skimmed.

## Question

- **v1:** Who supplies liquidity on Polymarket (makers, arbitrageurs, LPs, whales), found by
  clustering addresses?
- **v2:** What can the public fill record identify, and what can it not? Which archived
  conclusions survive, and which need a replication that normalizes for match
  fragmentation and accounts for mint and burn?

## Data

- **Scope.** Legacy CTF Exchange only (0x4bfb…8e), Polygon blocks 86,008,447 → 86,107,178.
  - The corrected window is **about 2026-04-25 17:09 → 04-28 00:00 UTC (2.285 days)**.
    v1 wrongly claimed the full week of 21–27 April.
- **Size.**
  - 13,356,931 `OrderFilled` records.
  - 43,116 tokens (v1 called them "markets").
  - 77,204 addresses with ≥ 5 attributed records.
  - An independent rebuild matches the counts within 0.1%.
- **Exclusions.** NegRisk markets are excluded by the contract filter. v1 wrongly said
  the NegRisk adapter was "inactive, 0 fills".
- **V2 cutover.** Cited as **2026-04-28**: after it, queries fixed to the legacy contract
  return nothing.
- **v1 extras.** The PMXT v2 archive (13.7×10⁹ events, 61,087 markets) supplied metadata
  and book snapshots.

## Methods

- **Attribution:** each record credits both the maker and the taker.
- **Six features:**
  - f1 log(1 + records per active hour);
  - f2 log(1 + mean record notional);
  - f3 side imbalance;
  - f4 HHI across markets;
  - f5 entropy over 24 hourly bins;
  - f6 log(1 + number of markets).
  - v1 describes its preprocessing two contradictory ways.
- **Clustering:** DBSCAN (15 configurations, ε 1.15–3.44, minPts 10/20/30); HDBSCAN
  (9 configurations, v1); k-means for k = 3–7.
- **Cohort thresholds:**
  - whale: ≥ $1M attributed notional;
  - high-frequency operator: intensity ≥ P95 and breadth ≥ P75;
  - high-breadth: breadth ≥ P95;
  - power trader: intensity ≥ P75 and notional ≥ P75;
  - episodic: < $10K.
- **v1 statistics:** Spearman correlations of archetype share with 22 metrics, BH FDR,
  BCa bootstrap.
- **v2 propositions:**
  1. Record counts depend on how many resting orders one aggressive order fills against.
  2. Fills do not identify quote lifecycles (placements, cancellations).
  3. One cluster does not mean one economic type.

  Plus rules on when role labels are justified, and a four-grade evidence scale.

## Findings (under the archived attribution convention)

**Clustering.**
- DBSCAN gives one cluster and no noise in all 15 configurations.
- k-means with k = 5 has silhouette 0.227 (0.21–0.23 for k = 3–7).
- HDBSCAN gives 49–213 clusters or 81–87% noise, and was rejected.

**Cohorts.**

| Cohort | Addresses | % addresses | % attributed notional |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Whale (overlay) | 68 | 0.09% | 28.0% ($184.2M; median whale about $2.4M) |
| High-frequency operator | 2,952 | 3.82% | 23.5% |
| Power trader | 6,738 | 8.73% | 29.9% |
| Active lower-scale | 2,062 | 2.67% | 10.6% |

| **High-scale subtotal** | 9,758 | 12.64% | **81.4%** |

- Concentration multiplier (notional share ÷ address share): about 6.44 for high-scale
  vs 0.083 for episodic, roughly 77×.
- **v1-only numbers, withdrawn or recast:**
  - Gini 0.932.
  - 17.9% of addresses → 93.2% of notional.
  - $4.77 mean retail fill.
  - 3,980 wash candidates.
  - 75/110 significant correlations (for example share × OFI ρ = +0.66).
  - Raw Kyle λ range ±7×10¹⁶.
  - 77.9% of activity in the three main classes was sports.

## Reusable definitions

- Source-record attribution.
- Fill-side vs quote-lifecycle observability.
- Discipline on role labels, and the four-grade evidence scale.
- Concentration multiplier χ.
- Signed markout A = s(p₍ⱼ₊Δ₎ − pⱼ), valid only when direction is defined.
- A replication grid on two axes:
  - windows: archived, week, month, quarter, full history, rolling;
  - attribution: as archived, match-normalized, without the direction feature,
    mint/burn-aware, transfer-only.

## Stated limitations

The paper cannot show:
- persistent types, because the window is too short;
- NegRisk or multi-outcome markets;
- independent trading decisions;
- the direction of mints and burns;
- entity identity;
- posted spreads, quote lifetimes, spoofing, stuffing or withdrawal;
- anything causal or about welfare.

**Future work:** run the replication grid and report cluster counts, silhouette, tier
stability and transition matrices.

## Critical assessment

- v2 is a candid correction. But every surviving number comes from a pipeline whose v1
  had the wrong window length and misread a contract filter.
- The window ends exactly at the V2 cutover, so it may be atypical.
- Attributed notional double-counts and depends on fragmentation, so the $1M whale line
  and the cohorts move with matching mechanics.
- Cohorts built from notional percentiles make "notional is concentrated" partly true by
  construction.
- The one-cluster result mostly reflects heavy-tailed, continuous features.
- The propositions are correct but nearly trivial.
- **Trust:**
  - propositions and the evidence rule: **high**;
  - window, scope and counts: **medium-high**;
  - cohort shares: **low-medium**;
  - the clustering null: **low**;
  - v1 correlations and figures: **low**.

## Directions it opens

- Run the replication grid on both eras **including NegRisk**: one decision per taker
  order (`OrdersMatched` / orderHash), with each fill labelled as mint, merge or transfer.
- **Order-lifecycle lower bounds from orderHash:** time from first to last fill, number
  of partial fills, fill rate per resting order.
- **Queue attribution:** join WebSocket level decrements to fills by orderHash, so
  consumed depth can be attributed to addresses.
- **Entity resolution:** proxy/Safe → owner EOA, plus clustering on the funding graph.
- Cohort markouts with correct direction (1 min, 1 h, to resolution): maker PnL and
  adverse selection by cohort.
- Weekly cohort transition matrices and wallet survival over the full history.
- Mint/merge share per market as a liquidity and quality metric.
- Surveillance flags: gross ≫ net, self-match, round trips linked by funding.
- Privacy-preserving public cohort aggregates with versioned contract and topic
  registries.
