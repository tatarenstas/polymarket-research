# Beyond Profit Rankings: Identifying, Understanding, and Evaluating Smart Money in Prediction Markets

- **arXiv:** [2610.34754](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34754), v1 26 Oct 2026, cs.CE. 13 pp in
  ACM format (WWW '27 submission style), appendix §6.1–6.4 with prompts and a hardware/config
  table.
- **Authors:** Huaiyu Jia, Shuo Sun (HKUST (Guangzhou)).
- **Code/data:** none released. Wallet and transaction hashes in the tables link to
  PolygonScan. The orchestrator ("MasEngine") is custom and unpublished.
- **Read:** full PDF text (all 13 pages, every table, appendix and prompt excerpt). Figure
  contents were read from extracted labels and captions.

## Question

Copy-trading tools rank "smart money" wallets by win rate and cumulative PnL. The paper asks:
- **(1)** which wallets show exceptional historical profit, and how concentrated profit is;
- **(2)** whether their returns reflect information, expertise, strategy or luck, and how
  they trade;
- **(3)** whether a follower can replicate those returns given latency, liquidity, price
  impact and fees.

## Data

**Sources.** The Polymarket Data API (category leaderboards, trading histories, PnL snapshots
refreshed daily) and a self-hosted Polygon Bor full node.
- Positions are rebuilt by joining `OrderFilled` events, ERC-1155 transfers and transaction
  receipts. Each event is labelled OPEN / ADD / REDUCE / EXIT, plus non-trade operations
  (transfer in/out, merge, redeem, convert).
- Resumable cursors track API pages, snapshot times, watchlist versions and block heights,
  and log unresolved gaps.
- How the double-recorded taker-summary fill is handled is not described. The paper says
  only "deduplicated on-chain orders" and "reconciled native orders".
- All data falls in 2026, so the sample is effectively V2-era plus early-2026 V1.

**Samples.** Each analysis uses a different sample.

| Use | Sample |
| --- | --- |
| Profit concentration (Fig. 1) | 1,541,104 "evaluable" wallets, 2026 through 17 Oct; PnL is an "estimate" whose method is not given |
| Sector allocation | 15,412 top-1% wallets, chosen retrospectively by PnL |
| Price and position analysis | 1,681 identified candidates, 6–13 Oct 2026: 971 trading wallets (3,380,953 buy/sell actions) and 985 observed wallets (4,763,410 observations) |
| Persistence and clustering | Fixed cohort of 1,101 candidates; 6,062,848 reconciled native orders; train 6–13 Oct, validate 13–20 Oct 2026 |
| Copy trading | Paper 13–24 Oct; live from 19 Oct 19:40 (UTC+8) to 24 Oct |

**Weekly PnL.** Weekly PnL for the persistence test uses the **provider-reported** cumulative
position PnL, including unrealised. It is not rebuilt from chain data.

## Methods

- **Smart-money screen (Eq. 1, Table 1).** A wallet u in domain s at cutoff T qualifies when
  both windows pass:
  - 180-day window: ≥ 20 eligible events and win rate ≥ 65%;
  - 90-day window: ≥ 10 eligible events and win rate ≥ 60%;
  - in both windows, net PnL > 0 and profit factor ≥ 1.2;
  - the share of events traded on both sides over 180 days is ≤ 10%;
  - the latest resolution in the 90-day window is no more than 30 days before T.

  Trades on the same event collapse to one observation, and only events resolved and
  verified before T count. A zero denominator makes the result "undefined" rather than a
  pass. The authors stress that the screen does not establish skill.
- **Statistical validation.** Poisson-binomial tests, BH/BY multiple-testing corrections and
  block-based (dependence-aware) nulls.
  - 2,000 Monte Carlo replications per synthetic setting, varying event probabilities,
    dependence and edge.
  - No count of real wallets passing the skill test is reported.
- **Persistence.** Logistic regression of next-week profitability I(π>0), on 1,088 wallets
  and 3,207 weekly pairs.
  - M0 is the base rate. M1 adds last week's profit dummy D. M2 adds standardised
    log-activity Z_N and log-|PnL| Z_P.
  - Expanding-window temporal validation with 2,156 held-out predictions.
  - Estimated by maximum likelihood (BFGS) with wallet-clustered sandwich SEs and 800
    wallet-block bootstraps.
  - Scored by Brier, log loss, AUC and 5-bin calibration. Standardisation uses training data
    only.
- **Behavioural profiling.** 33 overlapping rule-based labels, plus a separate K-Means per
  dimension:
  - activity (4 features), scale (4), price (5), focus (6), position (4) and timing (3);
  - K chosen from 1–5 using silhouette ≥ 0.15, minimum cluster size, 50 × 80% wallet
    subsamples, 50 parent-event bootstraps (median ARI ≥ 0.60) and frozen cross-week
    assignment;
  - preprocessing: winsorisation, log1p or square-root transforms.
- **Multi-agent explainer.** Qwen3.8-27B served locally (temperature 0.2), working from a
  frozen evidence packet.
  - Roles: Rules, Wallet, Domain and Market-Panel analysts, then a Skeptic, a Claim
    Verifier, an Editor and draft verification.
  - Outputs are JSON claims typed FACT or INFERENCE. Later evidence is tagged
    RESEARCH_UPDATE.
  - The explainer has no role in order authorisation.
- **Copy-trading system.**
  - Polls every 2 s, up to 32 blocks per batch.
  - Signals: source buys of the same outcome grouped within 30 s, with ≥ 10 USDC notional,
    and one of: size > 2× the source's 30-day median (≥ 10 groups), a position increase
    ≥ 50%, qualifying history, or an "experimental" opening.
  - Orders: fixed 1 USDC (live) or 1 pUSD (paper) Fill-or-Kill, with limit
    `p_cap = τ·⌊min(p_s+0.05, 0.95)/τ⌋`, entry in [0.05, 0.95), and expiry 300 s after the
    source trade.
  - Exits follow the source proportionally: `Q_sell = Q_f(1 − Q_after/Q_before)`.
  - Paper fills are simulated against the live book at execution time.
  - **Follower eligibility does not require passing the 180/90-day screen.**
- **Replication-gap algebra.** Under a static book, a follower buying after the source pays
  at least the source's average price (Eq. 5). This need not hold in a replenishing CLOB.
  Per-share profit gap = Δentry price + Δper-share cost (Eq. 6).

## Findings

**Profit concentration.** Of 1,541,104 wallets, 68.66% (1,058,094) have negative PnL, 30.33%
positive and 1.01% zero. The top 1% (15,412) take **89.2% of positive profit**.

**Sector mix (RQ1).** Sports and crypto dominate the top-1% wallets' daily buy notional, with
esports and politics episodically large and a sharp June–July rise in sports. The authors
call this participation, not verified sector skill.

**Entry prices (RQ2), by count of buys.** 23.63% are in [0.4, 0.6), 11.55% are < 0.10 and
11.19% are in [0.9, 1.0]. Buying spans the whole price range.

**Position actions (RQ3), as a share of observations.**

| Action | Share |
| --- | ---: |
| ADD | 55.3% |
| OPEN | 11.5% |
| Redeem | 10.4% |
| Transfer out | 9.9% |
| Transfer in | 7.1% |
| REDUCE | 3.9% |
| Merge | 0.9% |
| Convert | 0.7% |
| EXIT | 0.4% |

The authors read this as scaling into positions, and caution that the one-week window cannot
show holding to resolution.

**Persistence (Table 4, Brier).**

| Model | Full sample | Excluding zero-PnL weeks |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| M0 (base rate) | 0.2493 | 0.2480 |
| M1 (last week's profit) | 0.2306 | **0.2459** |
| M2 (profit, activity, PnL) | **0.2278** | 0.2470 |
| M2 AUC | 0.681 | 0.577 |
| Test observations | 2,156 | 1,625 |

- M2 improves on the base rate by about 8.6%.
- Last week's profitable wallets have **2.63× the odds** of being profitable next week. No
  CI or p-value is printed.
- The authors call the advantage "limited" once zero-PnL weeks are dropped.

**Behavioural clusters (Table 2).** Evaluable N per dimension: 602, 548, 587, 557, 479, 418.
K = 3, 2, 2, 3, 2, 2, giving 14 subgroups.

| Dimension | Silhouette | Event ARI | Time ARI |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Activity | 0.496 | 0.718 | 0.513 |
| Scale | 0.327 | 0.814 | 0.672 |
| Price | 0.361 | 0.751 | 0.511 |
| Focus | 0.413 | 0.631 | 0.472 |
| Position | 0.426 | 0.747 | 0.522 |
| Timing | 0.501 | 0.796 | 0.687 |

- Activity: an ultra-active group of 107 at a median 1,076 trades/day, against 2.29/day for
  the low group.
- Scale: median entry $582 versus $35.84.
- Price: 40.0% of wallets prefer high-price entry; one cluster puts 45.5% of buys at ≥ 0.9.
- Focus: 51.3% are domain-focused.
- Position: 25.9% batch-accumulate.
- Timing: 43.5% enter mostly late.
- Cross-week label retention: high-frequency 89.4%, low-price entry 34.8%.

**Copy trading (Tables 5–6).**

| Metric | Paper | Live |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Entry candidates | 2,896 | 1,384 |
| FOK orders submitted | 713 | 20 |
| Buys executed | 283 | 20 |
| Candidate-to-fill rate | 9.77% | 1.45% |
| FOK fill rate | 39.69% | 100% |

- **Follower price vs source** (higher / equal / lower): paper 201/61/21, live 13/5/2.
- **Mean price gap** per share: +0.01015 (paper), +0.00619 (live). The median is +0.010 in
  both.
- **Confirmation delay:** median 112 s live; 46–188 s on ten example trades.
- **Paper PnL** is +44.18 pUSD: realised +39.11, unrealised +5.07, on an entry cost of 693.46.
  - 44 larger entries made +75.73 and 239 smaller ones lost 31.55.
  - One ETH market made +56.07; without it, PnL is **−11.89**. That trade was blocked live
    by a data-validity check.
- **Live PnL** is −3.04 USDC on an entry cost of 20.36.
- **18 trades closed in both accounts:** −3.23 pUSD paper versus −3.19 USDC live, so there is
  no systematic paper/live execution bias.
- Fees on the ten live 1-USDC fills ranged 0.0000–0.0365 USDC, which is up to about 3.7% of
  notional (my arithmetic).

**Multiple-testing simulations.** With a correctly specified block null and BY correction,
the false discovery rate is 0.40–0.60%. It is 12.50–39.40% when event dependence is ignored.

**Multi-agent example runs.** One run had 15/15 claims verified and 10/10 draft blocks
supported (status "review required"). Another had 15/18 verified and 3/18 unsupported, and
failed validation. The authors note these are not accuracy estimates.

## Reusable definitions

- The Eq. 1 / Table 1 screen: event-cluster win rate, profit factor, bidirectional ratio and
  resolution-age recency, with "undefined" rather than pass on missing data.
- The position-action taxonomy: OPEN / ADD / REDUCE / EXIT plus transfer, merge, redeem and
  convert.
- The replication-gap identity (Eqs. 5–6) and the proportional exit rule (Eq. 9).
- The capped follower limit price `p_cap` with a 300 s expiry.
- Copy-trading funnel metrics: candidate-to-fill, FOK fill rate, price gap and confirmation
  gap.
- Cluster-stability protocol: silhouette, parent-event bootstrap ARI and cross-week ARI.

## Stated limitations

- Screen thresholds are prespecified and do not establish skill. Retrospective top-1%
  selection does not show ex-ante expertise.
- Price shares are by count, not notional. The panels in Fig. 4 use different samples.
- The one-week window cannot show holding to resolution.
- Confirmation gaps are not CLOB matching latency.
- The paper/live gap reflects signal selection and sizing.
- Copy trading is shown to be feasible, not consistently profitable.
- The logistic-regression run lacks a complete environment snapshot, and no generation seed
  was recorded.
- No explicit future-work section.

## Critical assessment

- **The screen and the trading test are disconnected.** Followed wallets need not pass
  Eq. 1, and the paper never reports how many of the 1,101 candidates pass the
  Poisson-binomial/BY skill test on real data. "Skill" is never tested on real wallets.
- **Persistence rests on inactivity.** Most of the Brier gain disappears once zero-PnL weeks
  are dropped: M1 is 0.2459 against 0.2480. The positive "persistence" is largely a wallet
  staying active or inactive.
  - Weekly PnL is the API's cumulative PnL including unrealised, so it is a mark-to-market
    measure.
  - The odds ratio of 2.63 has no printed CI, despite clustered SEs being computed.
- **Very short windows.** One training week and one validation week, with a 4–11 day
  trading test. Cluster stability is shown across one week pair only.
- **Copy-trading evidence is tiny.** 20 live fills of 1 USDC each. Paper PnL depends on one
  trade, and the result reverses without it.
- **Data plumbing is underspecified.** Fig. 1's PnL "estimate" has no stated method. The
  handling of the duplicated `OrderFilled` taker leg and of mint/merge legs is not described.
- **Editorial inconsistencies:**
  - the running header says "Author Name";
  - the text cites "Table 4" for the cluster results, which are in Table 2;
  - Table 6's note mentions "TBD" entries but none appear;
  - candidate counts of 1,101, 1,681, 971 and 985 are used in different places without a
    reconciliation.
- **Trust:**
  - profit concentration (68.66% losing, top 1% = 89.2%): **medium** (consistent with other
    work, but the estimation method is not given);
  - short-run persistence: **low–medium** (fragile to the zero-PnL filter, mark-to-market
    PnL, one week);
  - behavioural heterogeneity and cluster shares: **medium** (well documented stability, but
    one week and a retrospectively selected cohort);
  - replication gap (followers pay about +1¢ per share, with low fill rates): **medium**
    (mechanism sound, n small);
  - copy-trading profitability: **low** (n = 20 live; paper PnL driven by one trade);
  - dependence-aware FDR: **medium** (simulation only, but the warning about ignoring event
    dependence is well founded).

## Directions it opens

- Run the Eq. 1 screen, plus a dependence-aware Poisson-binomial/BY skill test, on full
  on-chain history for both eras. Report how many wallets survive and whether survivors
  persist out of sample beyond one week.
- Rebuild wallet PnL from fills, merges, redemptions and conversions. Measure the gap from
  Data-API PnL and leaderboards, and between realised and mark-to-market PnL.
- Build a population-wide follower-latency curve: the price gap and fill probability as a
  function of the delay after a source trade, from fills plus book snapshots from the
  WebSocket. Split by category, fee regime and maker/taker side of the source.
- Measure the follower impact of copy traders: excess taker flow in the seconds or minutes
  after watched wallets trade, and whether it moves price against followers.
- Label position actions (the ADD/OPEN mix) across all wallets as a data product. Test
  whether scaling-in predicts outcomes better than single entries.
- Compute a profit-concentration curve per category and month, as a market-health metric.
- Run cluster-stability checks over months rather than one week, keyed to on-chain
  wallet→entity clustering.
- Use an LLM explainer with strict evidence time-stamping (the paper's RESEARCH_UPDATE tag)
  as a template for auditable trade-attribution tools.
