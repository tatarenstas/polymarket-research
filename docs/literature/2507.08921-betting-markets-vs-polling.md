# Are Betting Markets Better than Polling in Predicting Political Elections?

- **arXiv:** [2507.08921](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.08921), v1 11 Jul 2025 (paper dated 7 Jul 2025), stat.OT. 30 pp: 19 of text, 4 figures, references, and Appendix A (list of pollsters). No later versions.
- **Authors:** Laurie E. Cutting, Sarah S. Hughes-Berheim, Paul M. Johnson, Hiba Baroud, Brett Goldstein (Vanderbilt: Peabody College; Civil & Environmental Engineering; School of Engineering; Institute of National Security).
- **Code/data:** the paper says the "Supplementary Materials" contain code and data to reproduce every analysis, but gives no URL. Polymarket data come from a Kaggle dataset (cited as Andrade, 2024); polls from FiveThirtyEight.
- **Read:** the full PDF text (pypdf), including Appendix A. The figures are plots with no numeric tables, so the results are qualitative readings of those plots plus the text. **The paper reports no numeric accuracy metrics.**

## Question

Did Polymarket predict the 2024 US presidential election (national and the 7 swing states: AZ, GA, NC, PA, MI, NV, WI) better than traditional polls?
- The motivation is that betting elicits "who will win" rather than "who will you vote for", framed as Wisdom of Crowds.
- The paper also asks what drives the national Polymarket price (which state markets) and how each source's forecasts behave over time.

## Data

**Polymarket.** Daily closing prices from a Kaggle dataset, for the national winner market and state-level markets.
- Read as "implied probability of Trump winning", covering April–November 2024 as shown in the figures.
- No market IDs, no token/outcome handling, no volume or liquidity, and no description of how the third-party "closing price" was built.

**Polls.** FiveThirtyEight poll-level data (the site closed in March 2025), national and state, from about 200 pollsters listed in Appendix A.
- When several polls fall on the same day and jurisdiction, their mean and SD summarise that day; the SD band is plotted.
- **The polling series is a Trump vote-share estimate (it "hovered around 45%"), but the paper labels and compares it as a "Trump win probability".** No conversion from vote share to win probability is described.

**Alignment.** By date. Days without polls are missing values, and polls are sparse at state level.

## Methods

1. **Descriptive:** overlaid time series (Polymarket vs poll mean ± SD), national (Fig. 1) and for the 7 swing states (Fig. 2). Event markers: 1st debate (labelled "Jun-26"), assassination attempt (13 Jul), Harris nominated (21 Jul), 2nd debate (10 Sep). "Accuracy" and "usability" (completeness, reactivity) are judged visually.
2. **Drivers:** a BSTS local-level model with regression (Brodersen et al. 2015). National Polymarket price = μ_t + βᵀx_t + ε, with x_t the 50 state Polymarket prices.
   - Spike-and-slab priors give inclusion probabilities.
   - Semilocal and local linear trend variants are in the supplement; local-level is "marginally better" (metric not stated).
3. **Forecasting:** a local-level BSTS without regressors, fitted to each series up to 8 cut-off dates (28 Jun, 14 Jul, 22 Jul, 11 Sep, 5 Oct, 18 Oct, 29 Oct, 4 Nov 2024) and extrapolated to Election Day with 95% predictive intervals.
   - Compared on whether the interval excludes 0.5 and whether the Polymarket and poll intervals overlap.
   - No proper scoring rule, no out-of-sample error metric, no calibration.

## Findings

**National, descriptive.**
- Polymarket favoured Trump at all but two points (May and September 2024). In October it peaked near **67%** and stayed above **55%** until Election Day.
- From October, Polymarket no longer overlapped the poll SD band.
- The poll series sat around **45%** throughout and "did not react" to events.
- Polymarket rose after the assassination attempt and fell after Harris entered.
- The authors note the October drift toward Trump coincided with large bets by one person across several accounts (the "whale") and with manipulation claims.

**Swing states.**
- Polymarket consistently favoured Trump in AZ, GA and NC. GA and NC were rarely below 55% and often 60–80%, while polls showed 40–50%; the authors suggest these "were never actually battleground states".
- NV and PA favoured Trump from mid-October, except early 3 Nov.
- MI and WI oscillated around the poll averages, with the largest dips on 3 Nov. The authors read MI/WI as genuinely too close to call, so polls were "potentially superior" there.
- Their summary: Polymarket "clearly" showed Trump ahead in 5 of 7 states.

**Drivers (Fig. 3).** The BSTS fits the national series with tight 95% CIs. **PA and MI** have the highest inclusion probabilities. Other states listed: NM, SD, KS, NH, WI, ND, AZ, NE. Inclusion values are only shown graphically.

**Forecasting (Fig. 4).**
- Polymarket behaves like a random walk (state variance τ² > 0), so its forecast intervals fan out. Polls behave like noise around a constant mean (τ² ≈ 0, σ² > 0), so their intervals stay narrow.
- **From about 18 Oct 2024**, the Polymarket 95% PI lies entirely above 0.5 for Trump, and the Polymarket and poll PIs stop overlapping.
- The poll mean never favoured Trump, and its PI straddled 0.5 except on Election Day, when it "incorrectly predicts Harris".
- State PIs separate from polls from 18 Oct for AZ, NC and GA, and by 29 Oct for NV and PA (PA almost by 22 Oct). MI and WI never separate.

**Interpretation offered.** Polymarket users differ from poll samples. They are crypto holders (17–20% of Americans own crypto), skew Republican and male, and were nominally non-US under the 2022 CFTC settlement, though VPN use was reported. Markets may capture groups that polls under-sample.

## Reusable definitions

- BSTS local-level ("random walk plus noise") as a descriptive split of a price series into persistent and transient variance (τ² vs σ²).
- A spike-and-slab inclusion probability for "which sub-markets drive the headline market" (state → national).
- "Divergence date": the first date when two sources' predictive intervals stop overlapping.

## Stated limitations (and future work)

- A single market (Polymarket) against an aggregate of many polls, so there is no comparable dispersion measure. Other markets (IEM, Kalshi, PredictIt) were not analysed; IEM reportedly favoured Harris.
- Trader count and demographics are unknown. One whale operated many accounts, US users came in via VPN, and crypto users are not representative.
- Manipulation, wash trading and the risk of influencing voters are discussed. The authors call for "hardening" through anti-manipulation measures, identity verification, transparency and audit tools, and mention blackout periods.
- Future work: replicate in other elections and events, and explain the MI/WI patterns.

## Critical assessment

- **Category error at the core.** The poll series is vote share (~45% for Trump, with undecideds and third parties in the denominator), but it is plotted and tested against 0.5 as if it were a win probability. A 45% vote share is compatible with a high win probability. The headline that "polls never predicted Trump" and "polls favoured Harris" does not follow; poll-based win-probability models (538, Silver Bulletin, The Economist) are the right comparison and are not used.
- **n = 1 outcome** (plus 7 strongly correlated states). There is no proper scoring rule and no calibration. "Accuracy" means being on the right side of 0.5 for one realised outcome, which cannot separate skill from luck.
- **BSTS adds little.** Extrapolating a local-level model gives a flat forecast at the last value with an interval that widens as √h. "The Polymarket PI excludes 0.5 from 18 Oct" just restates that the price stayed above 0.5 by enough. The drivers regression explains a national price with state prices of the same market, which are mechanically linked by arbitrage, so it is not a finding about voters.
- **Data quality not documented:** a third-party Kaggle daily close, no handling of token and outcome or multi-outcome normalisation, no liquidity filters. The "$3.7B wagered" figure is not sourced to on-chain data.
- **Factual slips:** Election Day is given as "November 4th" (it was 5 Nov 2024), and the first debate is labelled "Jun-26" (it was 27 Jun ET).
- **Selection:** the paper was written after the outcome and frames Polymarket's win as validation, while acknowledging the whale-driven October move without testing it.
- **Trust:**
  - "Polymarket favoured Trump nationally and in AZ/GA/NC/NV/PA from about mid-October": **high** as a description of prices;
  - "Polymarket was more accurate than polls": **low** (vote share vs probability; one outcome; no scoring rule);
  - "PA and MI drive the national price": **low-medium** (descriptive, mechanically linked regressors, graph only);
  - "markets react to events, polls don't": **medium** (true qualitatively, but daily poll averages are smoothed by construction).

## Directions it opens

- **A proper market-versus-model benchmark:** Brier and log scores for Polymarket state and national markets against poll-based probabilistic models (538, Silver Bulletin, The Economist), across 2024 and the 2026 midterms, many races and horizons. Use our own trade-derived midpoints (VWAP or last trade at fixed UTC times), not a Kaggle close.
- **Whale-adjusted price counterfactual:** use wallet-level fills to rebuild the October 2024 national and swing-state price paths without the identified whale cluster's flow (or with its impact removed using estimated λ). This tests the paper's open question about how much the Trump premium came from one entity.
- **State-to-national arbitrage consistency:** build an electoral-college-implied national probability from state markets (with a correlation model) and compare it with the national winner market price, to detect mispricing and lead-lag.
- **Reaction-speed metrics:** event-time response of state and national markets to scheduled and unscheduled news, compared with the lag in poll aggregates.
- **A trader-demographics proxy** from on-chain data (funding sources, CEX deposit chains, activity hours, cross-market portfolios) to test the "captures under-polled groups" hypothesis indirectly.
- **A dataset product:** a clean, documented daily and hourly price panel for all 2024 election markets (both exchange contracts), so that studies like this one stop relying on undocumented third-party scrapes.
