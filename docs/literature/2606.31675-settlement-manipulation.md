# Settlement Manipulation in Prediction Markets

- **arXiv:** [2606.31675](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.31675), v1 30 Jun 2026, q-fin.TR (cross-list
  q-fin.GN). The same paper is posted as an SMU working paper
  ([PDF](https://skbi.smu.edu.sg/sites/skbi.smu.edu.sg/files/Research/Settlement%20Manipulation%20in%20Prediction%20Markets.pdf),
  "Current version: June 30, 2026"; also listed at ink.library.smu.edu.sg/lkcsb_research/7913).
  133 pp: a 64-page body plus appendices A (proofs), B (volume), C (overlap × moneyness),
  D (Binance vs Chainlink), E (threshold sensitivity) and F (push-down cohorts).
- **Authors:** David Dai and Ruizhe Jia (Stanford MS&E); Shihao Yu (SMU Lee Kong Chian).
- **Code/data:** none released, and no repository is mentioned.
- **Read:** the full SMU PDF, extracted with pypdf, including all appendices. The proof
  algebra in App. A was skimmed, while its statements and results were read. Figures were
  read from their captions and axis values only. The arXiv abstract was checked and matches.
  A press report says Polymarket moved the crypto up/down markets to TWAP settlement on
  7 Aug 2026. That is secondary and unverified, and it is not in the paper.

## Question

Polymarket's 5-minute BTC up/down contracts settle on a price that holders can move by
trading the underlying. The paper asks three things:
- **(a)** Does such a contract create cash-settlement manipulation (Kumar–Seppi 1992), and
  what does that do to the underlying's price discovery and liquidity?
- **(b)** Who gains and who pays?
- **(c)** Which design lever removes the incentive? The paper argues for contract horizon,
  with an aggregating settlement reference as a second lever.

## Data

**Polymarket on-chain fills.** Polygon fills for the crypto up/down markets, with over
60M fills on the BTC 5m contract alone. Each fill records the block timestamp, both wallets,
the assets on each side (USDC vs an Up/Down token) and the amounts. From these the authors
recover price, size, direction and aggressor.
- **Duplicate records.** The on-chain log has one aggregate record per taker plus one record
  per resting order consumed. Volume uses only the aggregate record. Wallet positions and PnL
  instead sum the resting-side counterparty across both record classes, so each position is
  counted once.
- **Signing.** Flow is expressed in Up-token terms: buying Up or selling Down counts as bullish.
- **Volume** is reported as shares ($1 face) and as dollars.

**Polymarket order book.** Full LOB snapshots (best bid/ask plus several dozen levels) and
single-level updates from the market-data feed, covering only about the last 7 weeks of the
sample. The paper gives no source name. These data supply the Up-token mid, spread and depth
within ±5¢.

**Binance BTC spot.** Tick trades with aggressor side, and top-5 book snapshots at 100 ms.
Both are aggregated into 10 s buckets aligned to the 5m clock. Depth is measured within
±10 ticks ($0.10) of the mid.

**Settlement mechanics.**
- The 5m, 15m and 4h contracts settle on Chainlink Data Streams, a pull-based low-latency
  feed: a median across nodes of a median across aggregators of VWAPs.
- Strike = the oracle price at the open; settlement = the oracle price at the close; Up wins
  iff close > strike.
- The 1h and daily contracts reference a single exchange and are outside the sample.

**Rollout.**
- The 15m contract started 9 Oct 2025 and the 4h contract 15 Oct 2025.
- The 5m contract was created on-chain in Dec 2025 but sat dormant until 12 Feb 2026.
- Launch dates are identified from the trade record.

**Sample and periods.** 1 Jul 2025 → 8 Apr 2026, split into three periods:
- **P1** (no up/down contracts): 1 Jul–8 Oct 2025, 100 days.
- **P2** (15m and 4h only): 9 Oct 2025–11 Feb 2026, 126 days.
- **P3** (5m live): 12 Feb–8 Apr 2026, 56 days.

**Exclusions.**
- Three late-Feb windows when the oracle contracts stopped trading, and a full platform halt
  in March 2026. These cycles drop out of the Polymarket analyses.
- 10 Oct 2025 (flash crash) is excluded from the intra-cycle Binance figures.

**Live window.** Each 5m contract is listed hours ahead, but about 93% of trades fall inside
the 300 s live window. All cycle aggregates use only that window.

**Proxy check (App. D).** Binance stands in for Chainlink. The Chainlink strike and settlement
prints are available for 5,553 5m and 1,855 15m markets, mostly 16 Mar–10 Apr 2026. Binance is
matched causally (the last mid in the 10 s bucket ending at or before each boundary).

**Settlement for PnL.** Chainlink where the archive has it. Otherwise the last Polymarket trade
in [0, +30 s] after the close; trades converge to the outcome in about 10 s.

**Fees.** PnL is gross of fees. The fee is taker-only and proportional to p(1−p): it peaked
near 1.56% at p = 0.5 from launch and rose to about 1.80% at the end of March 2026. Makers
pay no fee and are eligible for rebates.

## Methods

**Model (Sections 4–5).** A Glosten–Milgrom spot market linked to a binary contract (Zhu 2014
two-venue structure).
- V = ±σ. An informed trader arrives with probability π. Liquidity traders are uniform.
- A manipulator enters the prediction market with probability p_M and pushes spot in the
  settlement window with probability α.
- The contract settles on the spot posterior mean plus uniform noise εξ.
- Makers break even, but the book cannot cross.
- Equilibrium selects the smallest stable fixed point. Assumption 1: ε < σπ < ½.
- Extensions add n ordinary spot rounds before settlement.

**Footprint, P1 versus P3 (Table 2).** On the (cycle, 10 s bin) panel, using bins 0–24 and 29:

  log y = bin FE + date FE + hour FE + δ·1{b=29}·1{P3}

- Outcomes: |net signed taker $ flow|, |10 s log-mid return| and depth.
- SEs are HC1, clustered by cycle.

**Reversal (Table 3).** For each lag j ∈ {−3…+3}, r_j is regressed on r29 (the final-10 s
return) and r29×P3, with date and hour FE and SEs clustered by date.

**Within-P3 identification by moneyness (Tables 4–5).**
- A cycle is near-the-money (NTM) if the Up price at TTM ∈ [10, 12] s satisfies
  |p − 0.5| < 0.10; otherwise it is far-from-the-money (FFM). About 4% of P3 cycles have no
  trade in that window and are dropped.
- The same activity and reversal regressions are run with NTM interactions, on both venues.

**Horizon test, 15m (Tables 6–7).** A difference-in-differences: (P2 − P1) ×
(cycles closing on a 15-min mark − other cycles).
- Cycle-type × bin FE absorb round-clock algorithmic flow.
- App. C.1 splits P3 into 6 overlap × per-contract-NTM groups. App. C.2 applies the NTM cut to
  the 15m contract alone in P2.

**Manipulated cycles.**
- PushIntensity = |Binance flow in bin 29| / median over bins 0–24 of |flow|. It uses no
  Polymarket price or outcome.
- The top decile (≥ 16.11) is labelled manipulated: 1,613 cycles, against 14,460 normal.
  Another 55 cycles have no Polymarket trades.

**Success test (Table 9).**
- The favoured-side price is p_fav = max(p_Up, 1 − p_Up) at TTM 10 s.
- Push direction = sign of bin-29 Binance flow, classed as same or opposite to the favoured side.
- The win rate of the pushed side is compared with that side's unconditional win rate in
  normal cycles of the same p_fav bin.

**Wallet PnL.**
- PnL_{w,c} = Δcash + N_Up·1{Up wins} + N_Down·1{Down wins}. Each cycle has its own tokens, so
  every cycle is a closed loop.
- There are 243,155 wallets, each treated as one trader.

**Trader types.**
- **Manipulator:** in ≥ 5 manipulated cycles with ≥ $2,000 aggregate PnL in them.
- **Market maker:** ≥ 85% of fills passive, ≥ 50 fills per active cycle, and median closing
  inventory |Up − Down| / gross ≤ 20%.
- **Retail/other:** everyone else.

**Loss share.** Each type's share of the gross loss pool within a cycle, weighted across cycles
by pool size.

**Hedging rebuttal (Figure 12, App. F).**
- Cumulative net Up position by cohort and cumulative Binance flow over the cycle, for push-up
  and push-down cycles.
- A magnitude comparison: the maximum MM loss versus the size of the spot push.

**Robustness.** A 4×5 grid of manipulator thresholds, n ∈ {3, 5, 10, 20} cycles and
$ ∈ {500, 1k, 2k, 5k, 10k} (App. E).

## Findings

**Theory.**
- **Prop. 3: price discovery falls.** Any interior p_M raises the residual variance of V at
  settlement. Two channels: the manipulator displaces informed flow, and her pushes dilute
  the information in buys.
- **Prop. 1 / Cor. 1: spot liquidity rises.** The settlement-window spot ask falls in α and p_M.
- **Prop. 4: equilibrium push.** α*₊ = 0 (no push after favourable flow) and α*₋ = 1 (always push
  after adverse flow). Interior entry p*_M ∈ (0, p₀). Profit (1 − 2σπ)p*²/(4p₀) > 0.
- **Prop. 4: zero spread with a rent.** The break-even prediction-market ask falls below ½,
  so the non-crossing book pins quotes at ½. The PM maker therefore earns a rent at a zero
  spread.
- **Prop. 5: who pays.** The liquidity trader's loss = PM-maker rent + manipulator profit +
  spot push cost. She is hurt through the spot maker shading its quote against Up holders.
- **Cor. 2: comparative statics.** Entry and profit rise with ε and fall with σ and π.
- **Props. 6–8: horizon.** A push pays only when the order imbalance is within one step of
  balance. Price discovery improves with each extra round. Entry probability alternates with
  the parity of n. The pivotal probability m(n) ≤ (1 − π²)^⌊n/2⌋ → 0, and profit → 0 with it.
  In the appendix, even-n cases have a PM ask above ½, so the zero-spread rent applies to
  odd n.

## Reusable definitions

- The P1/P2/P3 rollout periods, and the dormant-contract launch-date pitfall.
- The 10 s bin clock folded onto the 5m cycle (bins 0–29). "Body" = bins 0–24; the
  settlement bin = 29.
- NTM: |p_Up − 0.5| < 0.10 at TTM ∈ [10, 12] s, using only the freshest trade.
- PushIntensity, and the top-decile "manipulated cycle".
- The reversal slope γ_j of the lag-j 10 s return on r29.
- Overlap and non-overlap cycles (5m∩15m), with cycle-type × bin FE to absorb clock effects.
- Favoured-side confidence p_fav, and same versus opposite push direction.
- Per-cycle closed-loop wallet PnL, possible because token IDs are unique per cycle.
- The behavioural MM filter (85% passive, 50 fills per cycle, ≤ 20% closing inventory).
- The pool-weighted loss share.
- Strike-side agreement and the Chainlink–Binance basis.
- On-chain dedup: one aggregate taker record for volume, and the resting-side records for
  positions.

## Stated limitations

- Manipulators are identified from realised PnL, not observed intent: a wallet can profit in
  manipulated cycles by luck or by information.
- One wallet is not one trader.
- PnL is gross of fees; a net-of-fee version is left to future work.
- One contract, one platform, two months.
- Binance is only a proxy for Chainlink, with about 15% strike-side disagreement at 5m.
- The top-decile cutoff is a convention.
- The Polymarket book covers only the last about 7 weeks.
- Thin cells: 11 both-NTM cycles and 229 15m NTM cycles.
- Some hedging may remain inside the bin-29 buying.
- **Policy levers proposed:** a longer horizon; an averaging, auction or randomised close;
  position caps; taxing settlement-window spot trades.

## Critical assessment

- **No account link between venues.** Polymarket wallets are never matched to Binance flow.
  The "coordinated" spot push is inferred from timing and co-movement alone, and the manipulator
  cohort is never shown to trade spot.
- **The cohort is selected on its outcome.** Wallets are chosen for ≥ $2k PnL in manipulated
  cycles, so +$8.2M there is partly mechanical. Any decile of cycles would yield a profitable
  cohort selected this way. There is no placebo: random cycle deciles, or cohorts chosen in one
  half and evaluated in the other. The near-zero PnL in normal cycles is the more informative
  fact, and it ranges from −$1.43M to +$0.68M across thresholds.
- **The success test is partly mechanical.** Large final-10 s flow moves the price whatever its
  motive, informed sweeps and liquidations included. The baseline is the unconditional win rate,
  not normal cycles with comparable directional flow, so "34% vs 1%" mixes price impact with
  intent. The reversal and NTM concentration are better evidence against information.
- **P1 versus P3 is a before/after comparison** across different BTC regimes (Jul–Oct 2025 vs
  Feb–Apr 2026). The within-P3 NTM contrast is the real identification. NTM status is itself an
  outcome of the cycle's path, so it may correlate with volatility in that cycle, which date and
  hour FE do not absorb.
- **Logged outcomes drop zeros.** The |return| regressions have about 37% fewer observations than
  flow (738k vs 1.17M), consistent with zero-return bins being dropped. This is unmentioned.
- **Model and data disagree.** The model gives the PM maker a positive rent. Empirically, MMs lose
  in manipulated cycles; the paper explains this by quote withdrawal, which the model lacks. The
  model's spot-maker shading channel against Up holders is not tested.
- **"Flip" is loose.** The 65% vs 41% figure is the win rate of the pushed side in [0.5, 0.6), not
  a measured flip of a counterfactual outcome.
- **Trust:**
  - settlement-bin spike and reversal appearing with the 5m launch: **high** (large, precise,
    absent pre-launch);
  - concentration in NTM cycles, Binance depth up and Polymarket depth down: **high** (within
    period, two independent classifiers);
  - 15m attenuation: **medium** (the DiD is clean, but the NTM cells are small);
  - pushes overturning ≥ 90% favourites 34% of the time: **medium** (partly mechanical
    price-impact conditioning);
  - $8.2M taken by 821 manipulators: **low–medium** (selection on PnL, no wallet–Binance link,
    gross of fees);
  - retail bears about 70% of losses: **medium** (robust across thresholds, but the classification
    is residual);
  - hedging ruled out: **medium-high** (the magnitude argument is strong).

## Directions it opens

- **Rebuild the cycle panel for all coins and durations.** Use V1/V2-consistent fill dedup and
  extend past Apr 2026, especially across the reported Aug 2026 TWAP switch: a natural experiment
  in settlement-rule design. Test whether the bin-29 spike and reversal vanish.
- **Run the missing placebos.** Random-decile cohorts and split-sample manipulator identification:
  choose wallets in the first half, evaluate them in the second. Report PnL net of the p(1−p) fee
  schedule and maker rebates.
- **Look for the wallet → spot link.** Polygon funding, CEX deposit and withdrawal addresses, and
  the timing of Polymarket position-building relative to the push. Cluster wallets into entities
  before counting manipulators.
- **Build a live "settlement-risk" monitor.** Use WebSockets for the Polymarket book on the active
  5m/15m markets, Binance/Coinbase L2 and the Chainlink stream. Flag NTM cycles with thin spot
  books (overnight and weekends) and abnormal final-seconds flow, and publish PushIntensity
  and reversal per cycle.
- **Measure PM-maker withdrawal into the close**, by second and by moneyness, from the book feed.
  Test whether the NTM depth collapse predicts a flip.
- **Model outcome-flip probability** with a basis-aware rule. Settlement is Chainlink, not
  Binance; record the Chainlink prints for every cycle to quantify the 2.5 bps basis and the
  strike-side disagreement.
- **Check calibration of 5m/15m contracts at TTM ≤ 10 s**, conditional on spot-flow regime.
  Ask whether ≥ 0.9 favourites are systematically overpriced in thin hours.
- **Compare across venues and designs**: Kalshi 15m, Polymarket single-exchange-referenced 1h
  and daily contracts, and the post-TWAP contracts. Test the paper's horizon and aggregation
  claims.
