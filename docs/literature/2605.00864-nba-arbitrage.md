# Arbitrage Analysis in Polymarket NBA Markets

- **arXiv:** [2605.00864](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.00864), v1 22 Apr 2026 (only version; the PDF
  header is dated 5 May 2026), q-fin.TR. 12 pp including appendices A–B.
- **Authors:** Guang Cheng (corresponding), Jiaxin Yang, Haoxuan Zou (UCLA).
- **Code/data:** none released or linked.
- **Read:** full text (PDF, all 12 pages; arXiv HTML cross-checked). Figures 1–5 are images;
  only captions and the numbers quoted in text were available.

## Question

How often do executable arbitrage opportunities appear in Polymarket NBA game markets,
how long do they last, and how much can be extracted, for:
- **single-market** arbitrage (the two outcome tokens of one binary market), and
- **combinatorial** arbitrage between a game's Moneyline and Point-Spread markets?
The authors position this against Saguillo et al. (trade-based) by using order-book
snapshots.

## Data

**Context.** NBA markets ≈ 30% of Polymarket sports activity in 2025; NBA volume grew from
$51M (2024) to $0.89B (2025), ≈ 17.45× (Fig. 1, Oct 2024 – Feb 2026; source not stated).
A game event carries ~45 markets (~14 ML/Spread/Total, 30+ player props).

**Market universe (Table 1, counts and volume).**

| Type | Markets | Mean trades | Total volume (USDC) |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Full Moneyline | 176 | 10,412 | 251.2M |
| Full Spread | 769 | 367 | 41.1M |
| Full Total | 1,162 | 186 | 21.8M |
| Player Prop | 5,074 | 13 | 1.29M |
| 1H Moneyline / Total / Spread | 172 / 457 / 341 | 16 / 6 / 6 | 0.16M / 0.05M / 0.03M |

The period of Table 1 is not stated.

**Order-book snapshots.** CLOB REST `GET /book`, batched by event slug, 173 NBA games,
2026-02-04 → 2026-03-04, **75,088,497 snapshots** (also described as "collected from the
Polygon blockchain", which is incorrect for this endpoint).
- Polling cycle over all active NBA markets: **3.6–5.5 s** per market.
- Mean network RTT 171 ms; complementary tokens in one market timestamped 2–50 ms apart.
- A row is stored only when the book hash changes; unchanged tokens omitted, then
  forward-filled.
- Only Level-1 (best bid/ask and size) is used.
- Single-market universe: "3,042 markets" (not reconciled with Table 1).
- Combinatorial: 8.59M Moneyline–Spread joint states.

## Methods

**Single-market paths** (from Saguillo et al.):
- Long: Ask_A + Ask_B < 1; Short (mint-and-sell): Bid_A + Bid_B > 1.
- Because the book is mirrored (buy A at p ≡ sell B at 1 − p), a long signal shows up as a
  crossed synthetic short; only one path per timestamp is kept (deduplication).

**Filters (App. A.3).**
- Level-1 only.
- Bottleneck size > $10.
- Profit > 0 (NBA markets assumed fee-free in the sample).
- All snapshots after the game's physical end are dropped.

**Desynchronisation fix (App. A.2).** Updates within 500 ms are clustered and given one
timestamp, to stop a stale complement crossing a fresh price.

**Durations (A.4).** Forward difference t_{n+1} − t_n, capped by a "trust ceiling":
1,800 s pre/post-game, 300 s in-game. The last row of an episode is credited with the
market's median inter-snapshot gap (~4.0 s).

**Profit (A.5).** "One-shot": the single maximum realisable profit within an episode,
capped at a $100 budget, and uncapped (unlimited liquidity at top of book).

**Combinatorial (Sec. 4, App. B).** Favourite A, underdog B, margin Δ ∈ ℤ \ {0}.
- ML_A pays on Δ ≥ 1; Sp_A pays on Δ > h; Sp_A ⊂ ML_A.
- Execution: buy ML_A and the complementary spread token Sp_B (Δ < h):
  Ask(ML_A) + Ask(Sp_B) < 1. Payoff is $1, or $2 (the "Middle") if 1 ≤ Δ < h.
- Unified timeline = outer join of both markets' update times, forward-filled.
- Same filters, trust ceiling and one-shot profit.
- Ex-post check of whether the Middle paid.

No statistical tests or confidence intervals are reported anywhere.

## Findings

**Phase spreads (Table 2, median bid–ask in bps).** Pre-game 392.20, in-game 1,030.90,
post-game **7,532.65**.

**Single-market.**
- 37 raw episodes; 30 (81.1%) post-game and dropped → **7 in-game episodes**, 0 pre-game.
- Time in arbitrage 0.0001%; median duration **3.614 s**.
- Capped profit $210.19, uncapped $4,418.44, dominated by one blowout game where makers
  pulled quotes. Without it, the "representative" yield is quoted as 11.0% ($11.01 per
  episode).
- By type: Moneyline 3 episodes ($5.10 capped / $39.20 uncapped, median 3.964 s); Spread
  3 ($194.08 / $4,368.23, 2.652 s); Total 1 ($11.01 / $11.01, 2.276 s).
- 85.7% (6/7) could absorb a $100 order; no timing pattern within the game.

**Combinatorial (Table 6–7).**
- 523 candidate episodes; 233 post-game dropped → **290** (median 2.00 per game).
- Pre-game: 6,756,454 states, 11 episodes, 0.0034% of time, median 11.0 s; median yield
  309.28 bps; capped $32.42, uncapped $107.96.
- In-game: 1,834,885 states, 279 episodes, 0.1762% of time, median 16.0 s; median yield
  101.01 bps; capped $527.17, uncapped $1,924.79.
- Total: median yield **101.01 bps**, capped **$559.59**, uncapped **$2,032.75**.
- 17.2% of episodes last ≤ 4.0 s. Episodes concentrate in the final minutes (Fig. 5).
- **76.9%** of episodes could not deploy $100; average executable size **≈ 14.79** shares.
- **Zero** episodes paid the $2 Middle.

## Reusable definitions

- Long (Σ asks < 1) vs mint-and-sell (Σ bids > 1) single-market paths, with mirrored-book
  deduplication.
- Moneyline–Spread subset relation and synthetic short via the complementary spread token.
- Game phases (pre / in / post) with post-game exclusion; phase-specific trust ceilings.
- One-shot episode profit (max within an episode, not summed across snapshots).
- Bottleneck-size filter (> $10 at the thinner leg).

## Stated limitations

- 3.6–5.5 s polling: frequencies are a lower bound and durations an upper bound.
- One month, NBA only; generalisation to other sports, horizons or categories is open.

## Critical assessment

- **Single-market signals are almost surely artifacts.** On a mirrored book, Ask_A +
  Ask_B < 1 is the same as a crossed book in one token, which the matching engine clears
  immediately. Polled snapshots of two tokens 2–50 ms apart, then forward-filled and
  clustered within 500 ms, can create it mechanically. 2608.00666 (App. A) finds 20
  isolated mismatches in 308M WebSocket messages and drops this channel entirely.
- **Durations are bounded below by the method.** The forward-difference duration equals
  at least one polling gap, so a 3.6 s median "surviving a full polling sweep" is the
  polling interval itself, not evidence of genuineness. The text also says the durations
  are "all below the polling threshold", which contradicts this.
- **Combinatorial signals are more plausible but under-filtered.** The two legs come from
  separate polls up to seconds apart and are forward-filled, so "in-game, final minutes"
  is also when stale-leg artifacts are most likely. No check against fills is made (did
  anyone trade at those prices?).
- **Scope of the combinatorial search.** Only ML_A + Sp_B is checked; spread-ladder
  monotonicity (Sp at h₁ vs h₂), totals ladders, 1H vs full-game and player-prop
  relations are ignored.
- **Fees.** Zero fees are assumed; whether NBA markets carried taker fees in Feb–Mar 2026
  is not documented in the paper and should be verified per market.
- **Inconsistencies.** 3,042 markets vs Table 1's 8,151; 176 moneylines vs 173 games;
  "Polygon blockchain" vs CLOB REST; the 11.0% "median yield" equals the single Total
  episode's $11.01.
- **No uncertainty quantification**, n = 7 for single-market results.
- **Trust:**
  - single-market arbitrage is rare (7 episodes): **medium** as an upper bound, **low** as
    evidence of real opportunities;
  - combinatorial episodes ≈ 290, mostly late in-game: **medium** (sampling artifacts
    possible);
  - depth bottleneck (76.9% cannot fill $100; ~14.8 shares): **medium** (L1 only, deeper
    levels ignored, so size is understated);
  - post-game books are hollow (7,533 bps median spread): **high**;
  - no Middle realised: **high** as a count, uninformative as a probability.

## Directions it opens

- Re-run on WebSocket book streams (event-time, server timestamps) instead of REST polls,
  and validate every candidate against on-chain fills in the same seconds.
- A full sports-coherence graph per game: ML ⊃ spreads (ladder monotonicity across h),
  totals ladders, 1H ⊂ full-game, player props vs team totals; depth-walked, fee-aware.
- Who actually trades these gaps: wallet-level detection of paired ML/spread buys within
  seconds, their PnL, and whether they are makers or takers.
- The live-play repricing lag: latency of Polymarket books to scoring events (needs a
  play-by-play feed with timestamps), and maker quote withdrawal around big plays.
- Post-game "settlement window" microstructure: depth collapse, stale orders, and the
  game-end-to-resolution lag by league.
- Product: a sports coherence alert feed and a per-game liquidity profile (depth by phase
  and minute) for market makers.
