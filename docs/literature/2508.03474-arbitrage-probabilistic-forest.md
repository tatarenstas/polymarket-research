# Unravelling the Probabilistic Forest: Arbitrage in Prediction Markets

- **arXiv:** [2508.03474](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.03474), v1 5 Aug 2025, cs.CR (also q-fin.TR). 36 pp.
- **Authors:** Oriol Saguillo, Vahid Ghafouri, Lucianna Kiffer, Guillermo Suarez-Tangil (IMDEA Networks; Oxford Internet Institute).
- **Funding:** Flashbots FRP-51. No code or data release is mentioned.
- **Read:** the full HTML, all sections and appendices. Figures are images, and most distribution results appear only in figures.

## Questions

- **Q1:** What conditions give rise to arbitrage?
- **Q2:** Does arbitrage occur on Polymarket?
- **Q3:** Has anyone exploited it?

Arbitrage keeps probabilities coherent, and on-chain, non-atomic arbitrage is a form of
MEV. Checking every combination of markets naively costs O(2^(n+m)).

## Data

**Market metadata.** From the CLOB API (py-clob-client).
- 8,659 single-condition markets and 1,578 NegRisk markets (8,559 conditions);
  17,218 conditions in total.
- Markets resolved between 2024-04-01 and 2025-04-01.
- Where `end_date_iso` is inconsistent, the most frequent value is used.

**On-chain events.** Polygon traces via Alchemy, 2024-01-01 → 2025-04-01:
`OrderFilled`, `PositionSplit`, `PositionsMerge`.
- 86.6M bids: mean $135.6, median $8.29, maximum $2.48M.

**Topics.** 7 Polymarket topics assigned with Linq-Embed-Mistral embeddings; 92% accurate
on 100 hand-labelled markets. (The appendix names a different model.)

## Methods

**Definitions.**
- *Two-market dependence:* the markets are dependent when |V1×V2| < n·m.
- *Market rebalancing arbitrage:* long when Σ Yes < 1, short when Σ Yes > 1.
- *Combinatorial arbitrage:* price sums over dependent subsets.

**LLM dependency detection.** DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-32B enumerates the valid truth
combinations.
- Markets with more than 4 conditions are cut to the top 4 by volume plus an "other"
  bucket (more than 90% of liquidity sits in the top 4).
- Only pairs with the same topic and end date are compared.
- Then validity checks, an exhaustive subset checker and manual review.

**Opportunities.**
- Price is the per-block VWAP of *executed trades*, carried forward up to 5K blocks
  ("≈ 2.5 h") and then set to 0.
- Periods where any token is above $0.95 are excluded.
- Minimum profit is $0.05 per $; figure captions say 0.02.
- "Maximum profit" is the gap × the total token supply. Variants: 100 blocks, a $100 cap.

**Realized arbitrage.**
- Per address: buys, sells, and split-then-sell, ignoring bids under $2.
- Legs within 950 blocks ("≈ 1 h") count as one opportunity.
- Profit is the minimum tokens held across legs minus cost, with no fees.

## Findings

**LLM consistency.** On 128 election NegRisk markets, 81.45% passed all checks.
- Non-election pairs: 2,267 tested; 1 came out dependent, and it is weak.
- US election (5 Nov 2024):
  - 46,360 pairs → 1,576 flagged as dependent → 374 pass the checker → **13 true pairs**
    after manual review;
  - 8 ambiguous markets caused 1,469 invalid pairs.

**Single-condition opportunities.** 7,051 of 17.2k conditions, all "long" (Σ < 1).
Most of the potential is in US-election politics; with a $100 cap, Sports dominates.

**Within NegRisk.** 662 of 1,578 markets, about 100 opportunities each, average maximum
about 40¢ per $. Sports dominates outside the election months.

**Across markets.** 13 pairs with a median of 8 opportunities (one pair had 6,630).
Liquidity is low: average maximum about $100.

**Realized profit:**

| Leg | Profit |
| --- | ---: |
| Single-condition, buy below $1 | $5.90M |
| Single-condition, sell above $1 | $4.68M |
| NegRisk, buy Yes | $11.09M |
| NegRisk, sell Yes | $0.61M |
| NegRisk, sell No | $4.3k |
| NegRisk, buy No | $17.31M |
| Combinatorial (4 pairs listed) | ~$95k |

- Total **$39.59M** "assuming ε = $1 profit per trade".
- Top account: $2.01M over 4,049 transactions. One trade bought Yes and No below $0.02
  each, for $59k.
- About 1% of election opportunities were exploited. Exploited profit in Sports
  single-condition markets exceeded the election.

## Reusable definitions

- The outcome-space notion of dependence; dependent subsets S and S'.
- Long and short rebalancing.
- Holding Yes on the complement of S is equivalent to holding No on S.
- Split-then-sell as a synthetic short.
- Top-4 plus catch-all reduction.
- Only compare pairs with the same topic and end date.
- "Delta": blocks between placing and executing an order.

## Stated limitations

- Only strict two-market dependencies; n-market, weak or temporal dependencies (for
  example semifinal → final) are open.
- LLM loops and context limits.
- Trade averages understate margins.
- Execution is non-atomic.
- Mispricing against real-world probability is not studied.
- UMA and oracle resolution risk (the FIDE case).

## Critical assessment

**Price construction.**
- Prices come from **executed trades, not books**. Carrying stale prices forward and
  then setting them to 0 creates Σ < 1 mechanically, a large false-positive risk.
- *Reviewer's inference, untested:* Polymarket's Yes and No books are complementary and
  the engine mints across them, so a quote-level Yes + No < 1 within one condition should
  rarely persist. The 7,051-condition and $10.6M single-condition figures look more like
  sequential or inventory gains than riskless arbitrage.
- "Maximum profit" uses token supply, not depth.

**Event decoding and coverage.**
- `OrderFilled` comes from the exchange contracts, not the CTF contract named in the
  paper.
- NegRisk conversions are ignored.
- Markets created before 2024 are missing.

**Arithmetic.**
- Block-time conversions are inconsistent (5K blocks ≈ 2.5 h implies 950 blocks ≈ 0.5 h,
  not 1 h).
- Thresholds of 0.05 vs 0.02.
- Pair counts (13 / 11 / "5 pairs" with 4 listed).
- The components sum to about $39.69M, not $39.59M.

**Interpretation.** NegRisk buy-No ($17.3M) may be directional, not arbitrage.

**Trust:**
- taxonomy and definitions: **high**;
- LLM pipeline: **medium**;
- opportunity counts and sizes: **low**;
- the $40M figure: **low**;
- top-account identities: **low-medium**;
- "combinatorial arbitrage is rare and illiquid": **medium**.

## Directions it opens

- Correct event decoding across both exchange contracts and both eras, including CTF
  split/merge/redeem and NegRisk-adapter conversions.
- A quote-level, depth- and fee-aware coherence detector on WebSocket books (Σ best
  asks/bids per event), with the half-life and latency of opportunities.
- Classify executed arbitrage as atomic or same-block multi-leg vs inventory-based, and
  rebuild the $40M under strict rules.
- Who closes gaps: makers vs takers, bots vs others, and profit concentration across
  wallet clusters.
- Monotonicity arbitrage on strike and bracket ladders: crypto ranges, weather, tweet
  counts, vote margins.
- An event dependency-graph dataset (LLM plus templates) with a human gold set and
  published precision and recall.
- Overround in "augmented"/"other" NegRisk events, and the economics of No baskets.
- The resolution-risk tail: UMA disputes and non-exclusive resolutions.
- Product: a real-time incoherence alert service and a backtester with realistic queue
  fills.
