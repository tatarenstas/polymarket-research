# The Anatomy of a Blockchain Prediction Market: Polymarket in the 2024 U.S. Presidential Election

- **arXiv:** [2603.03136](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.03136), econ.GN. JEL D47, G12, G14, D83.
  - v1: 3 Mar 2026, titled "The Anatomy of Polymarket: Evidence from the 2024 Presidential Election".
  - v2: 7 May 2026, 41 pp.
  - **v3: 14 Sep 2026, 44 pp (25 pp of text plus figures, tables and an online appendix A–D).**
- **Authors:** Kwok Ping Tsang (Virginia Tech); Zichao Yang (Zhongnan Univ. of Economics and Law).
- **Companion paper:** Tsang & Yang, arXiv 2603.03152, which supplies the market-maker and
  same-entity screens used here. It has its own note.
- **Code/data:** none released. Logs were pulled from "the public Polygon RPC endpoint".
- **Read:** v3 and v2 in full from the PDFs, including all tables and appendices. Figures were
  read from captions only. v1 was not read.
  - **v3 differs substantially from v2.** It drops v2's YES+NO deviation half-life analysis
    (§5) and the intraday-seasonality figures, and replaces the tick rule with ledger-identified
    initiators.
  - v3 moves λ from hourly bins to 15-minute bins and adds naive-vs-corrected λ, a 249-market
    wedge analysis, the whale and concentration tables, and a VAR check.
  - **November figures changed between versions.** For the Trump market, v2 gives V^E 98.6 and
    naive OrderFilled volume 237.6 ($M). v3 gives 175.5 and 408.9. Both versions say the sample
    ends 6 Nov 06:46 UTC, and neither explains the change.

## Question

Polymarket's headline volume ($3.69B for the presidential event) was read as a sign of depth.
The on-chain ledger, however, mixes secondary exchange with share minting and burning. The
paper asks four questions:
- **(a)** How should volume be measured from `OrderFilled` and `OrdersMatched` logs?
- **(b)** How large is the naive overstatement, and does it generalise across markets?
- **(c)** How costly was the Trump price to move (Kyle's λ), and does the volume measure
  change that answer?
- **(d)** Was October 2024 one-sided whale buying, or two-sided disagreement?

## Data

**Logs.** `OrderFilled` and `OrdersMatched` logs from CTFExchange and NegRiskCTFExchange
(V1 contracts), taken from a Polygon RPC.
- Each record gives block, tx, maker, taker (a counterparty wallet or the exchange contract),
  both asset ids (0 = USDC), and both amounts in 10⁻⁶ units.

**Main sample.** The Trump, Biden and Harris markets of the 17-market "Presidential Election
Winner 2024" neg-risk event.
- Period: 5 Jan 2024 00:00 UTC (markets created about 22:58–23:02 UTC on 4 Jan) to 6 Nov 2024
  06:46 UTC (the Fox News call).
- The Trump market alone has 2,348,771 matched transactions, 5,085,439 `OrderFilled` logs and
  2,348,771 `OrdersMatched` logs.
- 231,885 addresses traded the three markets.

**Wedge sample.** 249 election-linked markets with 12.9M `OrderFilled` logs.
- 223 of them are neg-risk; one is a single-market event ("Will Biden announce resignation by
  July 31?", 16,876 logs).
- The other 25 are not described.

**Excluded.** Unilateral split, merge and convert operations, and legacy AMM scalar markets.

**Identities.**
- Addresses are not linked to entities. The companion paper finds only 355 addresses
  potentially controlled by a common entity.
- The 4 accounts of the "French whale" (Fredi9999, Theo4, PrincessCaro, Michie) are matched
  via display names.
- 109 "behavioural market makers" (heavy volume, inventory near zero, per the companion paper)
  are excluded from the concentration and disagreement measures.

**Initiator and direction (v3).**
- In each matched transaction exactly one `OrderFilled` record has the exchange contract as
  its `taker`. That record is the incoming taker order, and its signer is the initiator.
- This agrees with `OrdersMatched.takerOrderMaker` in every Trump transaction.
- Direction is signed as exposure to a Trump win: buy YES or sell NO = +1. A mint initiated by
  a NO buyer is signed −1.
- v2 instead used a tick rule on trade prices.

## Methods

**Transaction decomposition (Algorithm 1, App. B).** For each tx:
- buy_vol = Σ makerAmountFilled over fills with makerAssetId = 0.
- sell_vol = Σ takerAmountFilled over fills with takerAssetId = 0.
- trade_vol = min(buy_vol, sell_vol).
- If buy_vol > sell_vol, the tx involves minting.
  - It is "mixed" if the maker-side asset ids take more than one unique value; otherwise it is
    pure minting.
  - The excess on each leg is mint volume.
- Burning is the symmetric case. Minting and burning never occur in the same tx.
- The output is six components: {YES, NO} × {trade, mint, burn}.
- Buy and sell totals within 10⁻⁶ USDC of each other count as equal.

**Window measures over a window T, per share type, then summed over YES and NO.**
- **Exchange-equivalent turnover:** V^E = Σtrade + min(Σmint, Σburn).
- **Net inflow:** F = Σmint − Σburn.
- **Market activity:** V^G = Σtrade + max(Σmint, Σburn) = V^E + |F|.
- **Exact identity (v3):** naive `OrderFilled` volume = V^E + V^G = 2V^E + |F|.
- **Wedge:** naive/V^E = 2 + κ, where κ = (V^G − V^E)/V^E is issuance intensity.
- **Axioms (App. D):** V^E is the unique window measure satisfying single counting, routing
  invariance (exchange of s ≡ mint s + burn s) and "residual issuance is not turnover".
- **Window sensitivity (App. C.1):** October Trump V^E is the same under daily, weekly and
  monthly netting. With no netting (exchange component only) it is $268.4M.

**Wedge determinants (Table 7).** OLS of ln κ:
- Cross-section of the 130 markets with lifetime naive volume ≥ $0.1M, with robust SEs.
- Market-month panel (825 obs.), with and without market fixed effects, SEs clustered by
  market.

**Kyle's λ (v3).**
- Regress Δθ_t (the change in log-odds of 15-minute VWAP) on Q_t (net signed flow in $M), with
  an intercept and Newey–West SEs (96 lags).
  - Run per calendar month and as 30-day rolling windows with a 1-day step.
  - Empty bins carry the last price forward and set Q = 0.
- Corrected flow: v_n = YES trade_vol + YES mint-or-burn vol, i.e. the initiator's dollars.
- Naive flow: the raw `OrderFilled` USDC legs (2·trade + mint or burn).
- Prices and signs are identical across the two versions.
- Cost to move 0.50 to 0.55 = ln(0.55/0.45)/λ.
- **Robustness:** a monthly bivariate VAR(48) in (Q, Δθ) with flow ordered first; the
  cumulative 96-step response is rescaled to $1M.

**Cross-market λ.**
- Corrected λ estimated per neg-risk market (212 of 223 have ≥ 30 bins and λ > 0).
- log λ is regressed on log lifetime V^G.
- Within V^G quintiles: Δθ on separate secondary (exchange) and primary (issuance) flow
  components, with market fixed effects and market-clustered SEs.

**Participation.**
- Monthly counts of active addresses (maker of at least one filled order in the month) and of
  first-time addresses.
- An UpSet decomposition across the 6 shares.
- October net-buy concentration (top 1/5/10/20, excluding market makers).

**Disagreement.** Per address-day, net pro-Trump exposure x_{i,t} = Σ b·c·v. An address is
active if |x| > $1.
- **D_t:** standard deviation of x divided by mean |x|.
- **Headcount polarisation:** P^N = 2·min(N⁺, N⁻)/(N⁺ + N⁻).
- **Volume polarisation:** P^V, the same on side-level gross volume.
- **Side shares:** s^N and s^V.
- A 90-day rolling correlation of daily YES net inflow between Trump and Democratic-side
  (Biden + Harris) markets.

**v2 only.**
- Monthly AR(1) on δ_t = VWAP(YES) + VWAP(NO) − 1 in 5-minute bars, giving a half-life of
  −ln2/ln ρ × 5 min.
- Hourly active-trader seasonality by quarter.

  - 39.3% traded only Trump and 41.2% traded more than one candidate. (v2 adds 18.4% Harris

  - 71.8% touched Trump YES and 30.7% traded only Trump YES.
  - 19.5% traded all four Trump/Harris YES/NO shares (19.4% in v2). 15.1% traded only Harris

**October concentration (Table 11).**
- The whale's net Trump YES buying was $26.633M, or 15.1% of Trump F and 4.7% of V^G. By

  - top 5: $54.8M (31.1%);

**Disagreement.**
- The Trump vs Democratic-side net-inflow correlation rises around Biden's withdrawal and
  falls through September (debate on 10 Sep).
  - v2 says it then rose from about 0.17 to 0.76 in early October.
  - v3 says it stayed above 0.8 from 6 Oct to the call.
- Trump YES net inflow went from about $0.1M per day at the end of September to about $5M on

- D_t rises from mid-September. P^N stays high and falls only in the final weeks. P^V dips in
  early October and then recovers.
- Late October: Democratic-side addresses outnumber pro-Trump ones, but the pro-Trump side
  takes a larger share of dollars than of headcount.

**v2 only.**
- **YES+NO δ half-life:**
  - Trump: peaked at about 2 h in April and fell to 0.74 min (October) and 0.67 min (November).
  - Harris: above 30 h in March–April, then a few minutes after late July.
  - Biden: about 3 h, falling to about 8 min by July.
  - The Trump δ was persistently positive.
- **Seasonality:** Q4 activity concentrates in 09:00–20:00 UTC; the top 10% of traders peak
  at 14:00–17:00 UTC.

## Reusable definitions

- The transaction-route classifier (exchange, mint, burn, mixed) from `OrderFilled` asset ids
  and amounts (Algorithm 1).
- V^E, F and V^G, the identity naive = V^E + V^G, and the wedge 2 + κ.
- Window dependence: F is additive, while V^E and V^G are not.
- The initiator rule: the `OrderFilled` record whose `taker` is the exchange contract. It
  equals `OrdersMatched.takerOrderMaker`.
- Pro-Trump signing across legs (buy YES ≡ sell NO), and "corrected flow" as the initiator's
  committed dollars.
- Address-day disagreement measures D_t, P^N and P^V, and the side-share gap s^V − s^N.
- Active address: the maker of at least one filled order. Note that the taker's own record
  lists the taker as its `maker`.

## Stated limitations

- Whale identification relies on press reports, not trader identity.
- The welfare effects of prediction markets are open.
- V^E depends on the window; the paper reports monthly figures and says monthly and daily are
  not comparable.
- Unilateral split, merge and convert operations are excluded by design.
- Addresses are not entities.
- Future work: better trader-type classification as on-chain analytics improve.

## Critical assessment

**The headline "harder to move" claim is mostly an accounting identity.**
- Corrected λ exceeds naive λ because the same price changes are divided by a smaller flow
  (naive flow is 1–2× corrected).
- The $9.1M vs $15.6M comparison is a unit correction, not new evidence about depth. No one is
  shown to have relied on an `OrderFilled`-based λ.
- The level estimates (λ ≈ 0.022 in October) are the substantive result.

**Linear extrapolation.** The cost converts a 15-minute λ into the dollars needed for a
sustained 5-point move, assuming linear and permanent impact. The whale's $26.6M did not move
the price 15 points, so resilience and mean reversion matter. The VAR's 24-hour response
partly addresses this.

**Price construction.** Δθ uses the change in 15-minute trade VWAP, not the midpoint, so
bid-ask bounce enters. Because the bins carry the last price forward, empty bins create
Δθ = 0 with Q = 0.

**Primary vs secondary impact.** Section 3.3 states that the route is chosen by the matching
engine, not by the trader. A per-dollar impact difference between routes then needs a
mechanism, such as which side of the book is depleted or the timing of mints near news. None is
offered. Quintile medians differ by about 300×, so pooling within quintiles with fixed effects
is coarse.

**v2's half-life result, now dropped, was fragile.** δ was built from VWAPs of separate YES and
NO trades. Polymarket mirrors the YES and NO books, so the sum of taker-buy prices exceeds 1 by
roughly the spread. A persistent δ > 0 is what one expects from buyer-initiated flow on both
legs, not from an arbitrage gap. Its removal in v3 is appropriate.

**Inconsistencies.**
- November values changed between v2 and v3 without explanation.
- 249 markets = 223 neg-risk + 1 single-market event + 25 undescribed.
- The single-market dummy in Table 7 identifies off one market.
- v3 reuses "D" for both dispersion and side volume in P^V.
- The October correlation is described differently across versions.

**Selection.** Election markets only, all on the V1 contracts. There is no wash-trade filter.
Sirolly et al. flag 17% of Elections-category volume. They report 0% for the largest
presidential markets only because no threshold met their separation rule, so wash trading here
is not ruled out.

**Trust:**
- decomposition and identity: **high** (mechanical; checks exactly against the tables);
- wedge distribution across 249 markets: **high** for the description, **medium** for the
  determinants (n = 130, one market for the format dummy);
- λ levels and the fall over 2024: **medium–high** (NW SEs, VAR check; trade-price based);
- "40% cheaper to move": **medium** (true, but a units correction plus linear extrapolation);
- secondary λ > primary λ: **medium–low** (significant in one quintile, no mechanism);
- "two-sided, heterogeneous beliefs, not manipulation": **medium–low** (descriptive
  correlations; addresses are not entities; no counterfactual);
- participation counts: **high**.

## Directions it opens

- **Platform-wide volume layer.** Apply Algorithm 1 to all V1 and V2 (CLOB v2, from April 2026)
  fills. Publish daily V^E, F, V^G and κ for every market, and a "wedge" column next to
  Gamma/Data-API volume, which counts the taker's share quantity.
- **Wedge dynamics.** Compare κ by category and market age across the whole platform, and
  check whether high κ predicts low later V^E or thin books, as a market-maturity signal.
- **Combine with wash filtering.** Compute V^E net of Sirolly-style flagged volume, and test
  whether high-κ markets coincide with high wash scores.
- **Order-book λ.** Re-estimate λ with WebSocket midpoints, not trade VWAP, and with
  route-separated flow. Test whether mints cluster when one side of the book is empty, which
  would explain the primary/secondary difference.
- **Net inflow as a signal.** Test whether daily F by side (for example Harris NO + Trump YES)
  predicts price changes beyond the price itself, out of sample, across many neg-risk events.
- **Initiator-labelled trades.** Use the exchange-as-taker rule as the canonical taker label
  for both contract eras, and validate tick- and quote-rule signers against it.
- **Cost-to-move dashboard.** Show the live λ per market converted to "$ to move 5 pp", with
  intervals, for media-cited markets.
- **Concentration monitor.** Show top-k share of F per market and day, excluding market makers
  and clustering entities via funding links. Make the 355-address and 109-market-maker screens
  reproducible.
