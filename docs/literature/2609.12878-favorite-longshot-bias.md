# The Favorite–Longshot Bias in Prediction Markets: Evidence from Polymarket

- **arXiv:** [2609.12878](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12878), v1 11 Sep 2026, econ.GN (JEL D81, G14, G41). 51 pp, appendices A–H, 27 tables.
- **Authors:** Marcos Cardozo, José Ignacio Rivero-Wildemauwe (Universidad Católica del Uruguay).
- **Read:** all sections and appendices of the HTML. Figures were images, so only their captions and in-text numbers were used.

## Questions

1. Does the favorite–longshot bias (FLB) exist at both tails on Polymarket?
2. How does it depend on the aggregation unit, category and trading conditions?
3. Do longshot and favorite buyers form a recurring clientele, and do they bear losses or gains out of proportion to their spending?
4. Which explanation survives: misperception, risk-love or skewness preference, information, or market design?

## Data

**Source.** The Polymarket Users dataset v1.3 (Grégoire 2026; HF `vgregoire/polymarket-users`,
commit 91ddb961…, CC-BY 4.0), which accompanies Akey et al. (2026, SSRN 6443103). Gamma
metadata supplies the negRisk flag.
- The paper does not say which exchange contract versions the dataset covers.

**Coverage.** 2022-11-11 → 2026-03-29.
- 588.3M trades, 2.48M wallets, 729k child markets in 316k parent events.
- 615k markets in 255k events actually traded.

**Filters.**
- Complete fields, buyer ≠ seller, valid price and quantity.
- Two-outcome markets only (39 multi-outcome markets dropped).
- A wash screen on buyer counterparty HHI < 0.5. Wallets at or above 0.5 are 17.7% of
  wallets but 3.9% of dollars.
- Result: 586.1M purchases ($23.67B). The return sample is 560.9M resolved purchases
  ($22.49B).

**Conventions.**
- Each trade is counted once, as the buyer's purchase. The maker/taker flag labels the
  buyer as posting or accepting.
- Categories: Sports, Crypto, Finance, Politics, Tech, Culture, Weather, Untagged.
- Wallet PnL comes from the dataset's own summary. An independent rebuild for 9,781
  wallets agrees (Spearman 0.9926; within $1 for 96.4%).
- Some splits, merges, conversions and transfers are missing, so full positions cannot
  be rebuilt.

## Methods

**Return per purchase.** r = (y−p)/p, held to resolution, before fees and rewards.
- Longshot: p < 0.10. Favorite: p ≥ 0.90.
- Three aggregations:
  - R^O: equal weight per child market, dollar-weighted within the market.
  - R^D: pooled, dollar-weighted.
  - R^P: equal weight per parent event.
- Probability error: E = Σq(y−p)/Σq, in percentage points.

**Inference.** CIs clustered by parent event, with a Webb wild bootstrap for pooled results.

**Robustness checks.**
- An exact four-step decomposition between the aggregations.
- Cutoffs at 5/95, 15/85 and 20/80, and dropping the extreme price bins.
- By purchase quarter with resolution coverage, and with restrictions on the scheduled
  close.
- Excluding the 1, 5 or 10 largest events.

**Conditions.**
- Prior-trading quartiles, ranked within category × month × tail.
- Maker vs taker buyer, fee flag, shared collateral.
- Fixed-effects regressions on market × month × price-bin cells.

**Markouts.** After posted-offer purchases, at about 5 min, 1 h and 1 day, using the
last-trade price.

**Wallet clientele and experience.**
- Tail-demand deciles: a residualized 6-month tail dollar share, with expanding-window
  and cross-fit variants.
- Experience quintiles: average rank across four measures.
- Comparison cells: market × week × 1¢.
- Holding proxy: "no later recorded sale".

## Findings

**Headline FLB.**

| Aggregation | Longshot return | Favorite return |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Equal child market (R^O) | −6.30% [−8.38, −4.22] | +0.277% |
| Pooled (R^D) | −19.35% [−47.0, +8.3], bootstrap p = 0.184 | +0.83% (p = 0.001) |
| Equal parent event (R^P) | **+4.09%** | +0.392% |

- **Decomposition of the longshot gap:**
  - equal event weighting: +10.3 pp;
  - pooling within events: +0.06 pp;
  - weighting by longshot dollars: −23.4 pp.

  The headline pooled loss is therefore driven by a few large events.
- **Who trades where:** longshots are 18.0% of purchases but 1.2% of dollars ($273.6M),
  at an average price of 1.17¢. Favorites are 10.5% of purchases and 43.0% of dollars.
  Crypto is 67.2% of purchases but 25.2% of dollars.

**The full price curve.**
- Errors are negative in every bin from 4¢ to 45¢ and positive from 50¢ to 99¢.
- **The largest errors are in the middle of the range:** −5.86 pp at 35¢ and +7.28 pp
  at 60¢.
- Inside the tails, errors are only ±0.53 pp.

**By category** (equal-market / pooled, longshots).
- Crypto −14.8 / −12.6, with favorites +0.64 / +0.54.
- Politics −16.3 / −46.2, with favorites +1.04 / +1.42.
- Sports +2.4 / +18.1 (± ~93): no bias.
- Finance (pooled): −72.4.
- Weather: −25.2 / +24.7.
- Culture and Tech: not significant.

**Sensitivity.**
- Cutoffs: 5/95 gives −0.53% (n.s.); 20/80 gives −14.9%.
- Closing-date filters:
  - markets closing ≥ 90 days before the cutoff: R^O +30.7%, R^D −24.4%;
  - purchases within 1 day of close: −13.1% / −10.9%.
- Excluding the top events: pooled −15.8% (top 1), −14.5% (top 5), −10.6% (top 10).
- Event size: events with 2–5 children +39.5%; events with 26+ children −34.2%.

**Trading conditions** (pooled longshots).
- Prior-trading quartiles: −4.1, −6.5, −4.2, −28.7%.
- Posted vs accepted: **−3.50% vs −31.17%** equal-market (pooled −17.6 vs −22.2).
- Fee flag +10.0% vs no fee −20.8%.
- Shared collateral −25.8% vs none −9.8%.

**Markouts** for posted-offer longshot buys: +4.85% at 5 min, +6.96% at 1 h, +6.62% at
1 day, but **−36.3% at resolution**. Favorites: ≈ 0 short-horizon, +1.94% at resolution.

**Regressions.** Longshot return falls with log child count (−11.2, SE 1.4) and with log
prior dollars (−8.7, SE 0.7).

**Clientele.**
- The top longshot decile supplies 26.6% of next-month longshot dollars. Its membership
  is persistent: 68.5% remain after 1 month and 30.4% after 6.
- Its return is −22.0% versus −20.3% for others; it bears 22.6% of gross losses.

**Holding vs selling.**
- 63.6% of longshot dollars come from wallets that later sold the same token; those
  purchases net about +$0.2M.
- Purchases never sold lose **−53.3%** (−$53.1M).
- Completed buy-sell pairs return **+9.24%**.
- Wallets ever classed as longshot buyers have +$118M whole-wallet PnL.

**Experience.**
- Pooled longshot returns by quintile: −38.3, −44.1, −23.7, −22.3, −21.6%.
- The top quintile beats its comparison cell by +0.83 pp.
- It bears 58.2% of gross longshot losses and takes 77.4% of favorite gains.

## Reusable definitions

- Child market vs parent event.
- The aggregations R^O, R^D, R^P and their bridge decomposition.
- Probability error defined separately from return.
- Counterparty-HHI wash screen.
- Residualized tail-demand deciles, with a cross-fit variant.
- A four-measure experience rank.
- Market × week × 1¢ comparison cells.
- Posted-offer markouts.
- The "no later recorded sale" holding proxy.

## Stated limitations

- Returns are not realized PnL, because positions cannot be rebuilt.
- Fee and negRisk results are not causal.
- Markouts use trade prices, not quotes.
- Seller information and consumption value are not observed.
- Q1-2026 is provisional.
- Wallets are not people.

## Critical assessment

- **The headline number is fragile.** The abstract leads with the pooled −19.3%, which is
  not significant and halves without the top 10 events.
- **Filters flip the sign.** Equal-market results turn positive under closing-date
  filters, a selection risk from resolution timing.
- **Double counting.** Buyer-only accounting counts the same transfer twice: selling Yes
  at 5¢ is the same as buying No at 95¢.
- **Path artefact.** Cells weighted by price bin condition on the price path, so the
  mid-range S-curve could be an artefact. A time-sampled calibration check is needed.
- **Hold-to-resolution assumption.** Most longshot dollars are later sold, which weakens
  the clientele conclusions.
- **Statistics.** No multiple-testing control, and no CIs for the quartiles or markouts.
- **Trust:**
  - decomposition: **high**;
  - pooled favorite gain: **high** in sign;
  - equal-market longshot loss: **medium**;
  - pooled longshot loss: **low**;
  - Crypto/Politics two-sided pattern: **medium-high**;
  - Sports/Weather: **low**;
  - clientele persistence: **high**;
  - "recurrent buyers are not disproportionate losers": **medium**;
  - trading conditions: **low-medium**.

## Directions it opens

- Rebuild true positions from fills, split, merge, convert and redeem events across both
  eras, giving realized PnL and true holding to resolution.
- Two-sided transfer accounting: wealth flowing between makers and takers by price
  bucket and category.
- A calibration library with trade, dollar, market, event and **time-sampled**
  weightings, plus horizon-to-resolution surfaces.
- Quote-based tail costs from WebSocket books: effective and realized spread at 1–10¢
  vs 90–99¢.
- Mid-quote markouts by maker/taker and price bucket, as an adverse-selection metric.
- Fee-change event studies, using fee regimes that change within the same market.
- NegRisk conversion usage versus tail pricing and event overround (Σ Yes > 1).
- Wallet → entity clustering, then re-run the clientele and experience analyses.
- Better wash detection: round-trip loops and common funders.
- Extend the sample past March 2026.
- A product: a live per-event overround and tail-calibration dashboard.
