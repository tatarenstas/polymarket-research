# Price Dislocations, News Citations, and Epistemic Leverage on Polymarket

- **arXiv:** [2609.06005](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.06005), v1 5 Sep 2026, physics.soc-ph (also cs.CY, cs.SI). 17 pp, appendices A–F. CC BY 4.0.
- **Authors:** Hazem Ibrahim, Yasir Zaki (NYU Abu Dhabi).
- **Replication repository:** https://github.com/hazemibrahim97/epistemic-leverage-replication. It includes scripts, seeds, the 756-cell grid, aggregate events, the pre-specified plan, annotation guidelines and gold labels. Article text, wallet labels and the "cheapest target" tables are withheld.
- **Read:** full HTML text in two chunks. Only the top rows of Table 11 were cut off.

## Questions

Newsrooms now embed market odds: Polymarket with Dow Jones, Kalshi with CNN, CNBC and Fox.
- **RQ1:** Is a price dislocation followed by more news citation of that market?
- **RQ2:** Does citation follow move size, liquidity, or the market's prominence?
- **RQ3:** Are some markets both cheap to move and often cited ("epistemic leverage")?

## Data

**Trades.** Polygon `OrderFilled` logs: 173.7M signed fills, 224,637 tokens, 23,563
markets.
- Trade direction matches the Akey et al. (2026) panel on 7,848 of 7,848 fills.
- 2024 is the discovery period and 2025 the confirmation period. The last 48 h before
  resolution are excluded.
- Hourly price (implied Yes) and buy pressure ($ bought − $ sold).

**News.**
- GDELT → 6,990 English articles that mention a prediction-market venue.
- Regex search finds 1,826 candidate sentences; 1,582 are real odds citations, and 918
  can be tied to a specific market (905 Polymarket).
- Matching to markets uses rare-word overlap, dates, named entities and direction.
- An LLM (Claude Sonnet 5) pre-labels; two human coders label 303 items.
- Precision 0.987 (citation) and 0.976 (attribution); inter-coder agreement AC1
  0.96/0.93.
- Recall is checked on only 21 pairs.

## Methods

**Dislocation:** a move of ≥ 5pp (10pp as a robustness check) with buy pressure in the
top 10% of the market's trailing 30-day distribution. Adjacent intervals are merged.
- 44,976 events in 9,590 markets.
- The news panel has 14,558 market-weeks: 8,669 with a dislocation and 5,889 baseline.

**Citation intensity:** confirmed citations divided by shortlisted candidate articles.
τ_cite is a self-controlled log rate ratio (pseudocount 0.5, exposure-weighted) over a
3-day window, plus 1- and 7-day windows.

**Inference:** 1,000 permutations within topic-week, a within-market shuffle, and a
matched comparison.

**Selection model:** a logit over 16,236 events with category fixed effects and
market-clustered SEs. Predictors: |Δp|, log C(5), log volume before the move.

**Price impact:** Δp = α_i + λ_i·q on hours outside dislocations. λ counts as
"detectable" when it is positive and significant. Cost to move k pp: C(k) = k/λ.

**Epistemic leverage:** L_g = median C(5) / P(cited within 7 d | group g).

**Pre-registered design.**
- A 756-cell grid.
- BH correction over 7 categories.
- Replication requires the same sign in both years and q < 0.05 in 2025.
- TOST for the liquidity coefficient with |β| < 0.1.

## Findings

**Main effect.**
- τ_cite = 0.283, p = 0.001: about ×1.33 more citations after a dislocation.
- Poisson IRR 2.48; negative binomial 2.55; matched contrast 0.434.
- About 18% of candidate articles cite odds within 3 days.
- **Timing of citations:** excluding same-day citations gives τ = 0.239 (exploratory).
  74% of first citations come after the dislocation and about 25% the same day.

**By category** (2024 / 2025 → replicates?):
- Sports +0.91 / +0.74: replicates.
- Politics +0.44 / +0.39: replicates.
- Finance +0.21 / +0.41: replicates.
- Crypto +0.28 / +0.02: replicates.
- Culture, Weather and Tech: do not replicate; Tech's sign flips.

**Selection.** Prominence β = 0.610, liquidity cost β = 0.301, move size β = 0.159, all
significant. The hypothesis that salience matters more than liquidity is rejected.

**Price impact.**
- λ is detectable for 43% of 11,898 tokens.
- Median C(5) = **$21.8k**, C(10) = $43.5k. Among dislocation events, median C(5) is
  $16.5k.
- C(5) by category: Culture $6.8k, Tech $8.1k, Politics $20.8k, Crypto $24.3k,
  Finance $27.8k, Sports $110k.

**Leverage.**
- By category: Culture $293k, Finance $401k, Politics $455k, Crypto $1.05M,
  Tech $1.20M, Sports $5.58M.
- By prominence quintile: $0.67–1.0M. Cost rises ×11 and citation rate ×12.5, so the
  ratio is roughly flat.

**Partnerships (exploratory).** Among first citations, 613 come from unaffiliated
outlets and 0 from Polymarket partners. Too few to analyse.

## Reusable definitions

- Dislocation rule: ≥ 5pp with flow in the top 10% of the trailing 30-day distribution.
- Citation intensity and the self-controlled τ_cite.
- Linear λ with C(k) = k/λ.
- Epistemic leverage.
- A taxonomy of odds citation and attribution.
- Discovery/confirmation split by year, with a pre-registered grid.

## Stated limitations

- The design is observational, with shared news shocks; no causal claim is made.
- λ is not order-book depth.
- English-only GDELT corpus.
- Recall of the matcher is barely measured.
- One platform ecosystem.

**Future work:** quasi-experiments using outages and newsroom integration dates;
exposure experiments; publishing flow alongside odds.

## Critical assessment

- **Causal direction and the corpus.** News can drive both the price move and the
  citation, and the "strictly later" variant was not pre-registered. The corpus includes
  only venue-aware articles.
- **Statistics and the leverage metric.**
  - Many p-values sit at the permutation floor of 0.001; Crypto 2025 has τ 0.019 with
    p 0.001.
  - Leverage is a ratio of medians, built on a selected 43% of tokens and a linear,
    endogenous λ.
  - Liquidity and prominence are collinear.
- **Coverage.** 173.7M fills is a subset of platform activity.
- **Trust:**
  - the dislocation → citation association: **medium**;
  - prominence as the main predictor: **medium-high**;
  - leverage levels: **low-medium**;
  - price-impact costs: **medium**;
  - matcher precision: **high**.

## Directions it opens

- Platform-wide dislocation detection across both eras, plus a live WebSocket alert feed
  with flow percentiles.
- Depth-based cost-to-move from order-book snapshots, compared with the trade-based λ.
- Who causes dislocations (new, concentrated or repeat wallets), and whether they revert
  afterwards.
- A news ↔ market linkage dataset: extend the released matcher to non-English sources
  and other venues.
- Event studies on partnership integration dates (Dow Jones, Jan 2026) and on API or
  display outages.
- Reversion and profit after a dislocation, to separate manipulation from information.
- A "citability" score (prominence × cost) on a market-quality dashboard.
- Whether cited prices are better or worse calibrated than uncited ones.
