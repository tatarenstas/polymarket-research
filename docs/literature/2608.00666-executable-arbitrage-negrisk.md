# Executable Arbitrage and Market Efficiency in Prediction Markets

- **arXiv:** [2608.00666](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.00666), v1 1 Aug 2026 (only version), cs.CE. 23 pp
  including appendices A–G.
- **Authors:** Jonas Gebele, Timm Mutzel, Florian Matthes (Technical University of Munich).
- **Code/data:** none released. Inputs are third-party: the Wang et al. HF dataset
  `SII-WANGZJ/Polymarket_data` (events, `OrderFilled`), Goldsky subgraphs, PolygonScan, and
  the pmxt Orderbook Archive v2 (L2 books). Prototype adapter code is described as
  pseudocode only.
- **Read:** full text (PDF, all 23 pages, plus the arXiv HTML to cross-check). Figures read
  from axis text and captions. **Source defect:** several sentences are cut off after a
  percentage figure in both PDF and HTML (an unescaped `%` in the LaTeX source), so a few
  shares are missing (marked "[cut]" below).

## Question

Within a winner-takes-all (NegRisk) event, payoff identities imply no-arbitrage bounds,
but a bound is only *enforceable before settlement* if the protocol offers a matching
position transformation. Polymarket's NegRisk Adapter converts NO-side portfolios into
collateral + complementary YES before resolution; there is no reverse (YES→NO) operation.
The paper asks whether this asymmetry shows up in:
- **I1** incidence of positive payoff-bound violations (fewer on the supported NO side);
- **I2** persistence (shorter on the supported side);
- **I3** realized exploitation (concentrated in converter-enabled strategies).
It then prototypes a bidirectional adapter.

## Data

Three non-overlapping panels, analysed separately (not matched at opportunity level).

**FPMM panel (pre-CLOB AMM era).** 51 mutually exclusive, exhaustive outcome sets, 235
binary markets, ~53,000 trades. Groups were derived from event metadata and wording and
hand-validated against resolution rules (exactly one outcome can resolve YES). Trades
and pool states rebuilt from Polygon RPC.

**CLOB transaction panel (actor level).** 32,702 events, 259M trades, events listed
through 2025-12-31. `OrderFilled` and metadata from the Wang et al. HF dataset; Adapter
conversions and CTF splits/merges from Goldsky subgraphs and parsed logs, cross-checked on
PolygonScan. An **actor** = the address or Polymarket proxy wallet to which fills are
attributed. Exchange-contract versions are not discussed.

**CLOB L2 panel (market state).** pmxt archive, hour-stratified sample, 2026-04-14 00:00 →
2026-05-19 06:00 UTC (L2 is "available only from April 2026"). Each sampled hour is an
independent window: books initialised from snapshots, then all incremental updates
replayed, giving event-time book states. The number of sampled hours is not reported.
Footnote: the window is to be extended "by 24 observation periods".

**WebSocket validation (App. A).** Own capture of the market WebSocket, 2026-05-08 15:00 →
2026-05-15 15:00 UTC, 308,416,666 messages.

**Fill interpretation.** Raw `OrderFilled` does not normalise economic exposure for
split- and merge-assisted matches; fills are read jointly with CTF operations in the same
transaction.

## Methods

**Payoff identities.** N_i ≡ 1 − Y_i; Σ_Q Y_i ≡ 1; for any S ⊆ Q:
Σ_S N_k ≡ (|S|−1)·1 + Σ_{Q\S} Y_j (eq. 1). Complete-basket edges:
Δ_settle_Y = 1 − Σ a(Y_i), Δ_settle_N = (|Q|−1) − Σ a(N_i).

**Fee-adjusted directional edges** at each book state (a = executable cost, b = executable
proceeds, depth walked jointly across all legs; FPMM trades simulated on pool state):
- NO→Y: Δ = (|S|−1) + b(Y_S̄) − a(N_S) − f (Adapter-supported);
- Y→NO: Δ = b(N_S) − a(Y_S̄) − (|S|−1) − f (unsupported).
The largest edge over subsets is kept per event, direction and update. Taker execution
is assumed for every leg (conservative).

**Episodes and persistence.** Episode = maximal run of consecutive states with max edge
> 0; a state lasts until the next update. CLOB episodes visible in both the first and the
last 5 minutes of an hour window are "window-spanning" (≥ 50 min) and excluded from the
exact-duration Kaplan-style empirical survival curves.

**Converter-enabled exploitation.** Unit = conversion bundle (actor, event, NO inputs,
quantity, collateral released, YES returned); calls split when inputs/quantities differ.
- Inputs matched to CLOB buys or split-minted NO in the **5 blocks (~10 s)** before the
  conversion; all inputs must be covered; partial baskets excluded; oversized buys
  allocated pro rata.
- Split-minted NO valued at an imputed contemporaneous bid: spread assumed 1¢ at
  half-cent mids, 2¢ at integer-cent mids, 0.1¢ adjustment for mids < 3¢ or > 97¢.
- Returned YES tracked for **150 blocks (~5 min)**; sales/redemptions at realised proceeds,
  merged/residual YES at the imputed ask.
- Π_conv = V_collateral + V_YES − C_in (part realised, part mark-to-market).

**Settlement-based exploitation.** Actor inventories rebuilt from fills, sales, splits and
merges. A complete YES or NO basket over all active outcomes; matched quantity = min leg;
FIFO lots; formation time ≤ **10 minutes**. Π_Y = q − C_Y, Π_N = (n−1)q − C_N (profit
locked in if held to resolution). Converted quantities removed to avoid double counting.
Robustness: other time thresholds and LIFO (results not tabulated). Gas omitted (relayer).

**Anomaly exclusion.** 75 conversions by 3 addresses, 2025-12-12 22:07–22:14 UTC; robustness
via thresholds on return-on-cost and core-to-total profit share (results not tabulated).

**Protocol prototype.** `convertYESPositions` (Alg. 2) mints wrapped collateral, splits,
burns the complete YES set and returns the NO basket; gas benchmarked against
`convertPositions` for 5–196 outcomes. Hyperliquid composition (Alg. 3) and Kalshi
"Collateral Return" are compared qualitatively.

## Findings

**Incidence (Fig. 2 insets, episode counts positive / negative).**

| Panel | YES side (+ / −) | NO side (+ / −) |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| FPMM | 319 / 333 | 406 / 412 |
| CLOB L2 | **2,098** / 2,687 | **36** / 1,024 |

Edges per share (after fees) span about ±0.4 in FPMM but about ±0.04 in CLOB.

**Persistence.**
- FPMM: YES and NO survival curves overlap; ~40[cut] episodes open beyond 50 min, a
  non-negligible share over 1 day (Fig. 3: YES n = 361, NO n = 444).
- CLOB: of 624 "observed episodes", 366 (58.7%[cut]) are window-spanning (≥ 50 min).
  Of the remaining 258, median duration **16.15 s** (253 YES) vs **7.99 s** (5 NO). The
  authors say n = 5 does not support a distributional comparison.

**Realized exploitation (total ≈ 1.118M USDC).**
- Converter-enabled: **1.086M USDC** (≈ 97%). Full-set NO→collateral core: 205,531 USDC
  (18.9%); the rest is partial conversions and returned-YES management.
- Settlement-based: **32,283 USDC** total.
  - FPMM: market-state benchmark (max fee-adjusted edge per event, summed) 5,185 USDC;
    actor reconstruction finds 67 profitable baskets, 19 events, 35 actors, 3,639 USDC
    (FIFO). Repeat actors 70[cut]; one actor 76[cut].
  - CLOB: 5,923 profitable baskets, 28,644 USDC — YES 14,199 (4,014 baskets), NO
    14,445 (1,909 baskets). NO baskets could have been converted; why not is unexplained.
- Converter profit first appears January 2024 (Adapter deployed 2023-11-28). Top 10
  addresses take 75[cut] of converter profit (Fig. 4 names 9 address prefixes).
- Excluded cluster: **381,748 USDC in 7 minutes** from 75 conversions; mids ~50¢ before
  and after; read as likely related-party value transfer through temporarily populated
  books (addresses 0x8E9E…38e4, 0x5F39…a78c, 0xcd91…32b8).
- Maker-assisted conversions ("approximately 80[cut]") are more frequent and more often
  profitable; all-taker conversions are rarer but larger on average; weekly all-taker
  share mostly ≤ 40[cut] from late 2024 through 2025.
- Median profit per conversion: ~1 USDC through Jul 2024 → ~0.20 USDC late 2024–2025 →
  ~0.08 USDC early 2026, with a persistent negative lower tail (read as competition).

**Comparison with Saguillo et al.** On the same 2024-04-01 → 2025-04-01 resolution window,
this paper's mechanism-linked criteria give **$291,424**, versus Saguillo's $10.6M
(same-condition) + $29M (NegRisk rebalancing). Attributed to different estimands.

**Same-condition YES/NO arbitrage is an artifact (App. A).** Across 308M WebSocket
messages, YES and NO books were exact mirrors except 20 isolated single-pair
inconsistencies; the channel is excluded from the estimand.

**Protocol prototype (App. C).** Reverse path costs a constant **+63,094/63,095 gas** over
the existing path at every size; total gas grows from ~0.64–0.70M (5 outcomes) to ~25.3M
(196 outcomes). A reverse adapter needs a finalised *active-outcome* set, because a
placeholder's NO is collateral-like and returning it would create value.

## Reusable definitions

- **Payoff-space vs protocol-executable no-arbitrage.**
- **NO→Y and Y→NO subset edges** (fee-adjusted, depth-walked, max over subsets).
  Translation for a book with mirrored YES/NO: with S = Q, the NO-side edge is
  Σ b(Y_i) − 1 (sum of YES bids above 1, closable now by converting), and the YES-side
  edge is 1 − Σ a(Y_i) (sum of YES asks below 1, closable only at settlement).
- **Violation episode**, window-spanning episode, empirical survival of durations.
- **Conversion bundle** with 5-block input / 150-block output windows; full-set "core"
  vs residual profit.
- **Complete-basket formation** (min-leg quantity, FIFO, ≤ 10 min).
- Midpoint→quote imputation rule (1¢ / 2¢ / 0.1¢).

## Stated limitations

- Panels cannot be matched opportunity-by-opportunity (no historical L2 for the
  transaction sample); FPMM vs CLOB is descriptive, not causal.
- Hour-stratified L2 hides durations ≥ 50 min; NO-side duration sample is 5.
- Episode closure need not be caused by arbitrage.
- Π_conv mixes realised and imputed values; why NO baskets were not converted is
  conjecture.
- The reverse adapter requires active-outcome state management; large outcome sets stay
  gas-constrained.
- Future: extend the L2 window by 24 periods.

## Critical assessment

- **Count inconsistencies.** CLOB positive episodes are 2,098 + 36 = 2,134 (Fig. 2) but
  persistence uses "624 observed episodes"; FPMM survival n (361 + 444 = 805) exceeds the
  positive-episode counts (319 + 406 = 725). Neither gap is explained.
- **Truncated shares.** Several concentration and maker-share figures are lost to the
  `%` defect; they cannot be cited.
- **Identification of I1.** The YES-side condition (Σ YES asks < 1) is also what rational
  discounting for capital lock-up produces in long-dated events (the authors' own
  companion paper on settlement discounts). Incidence asymmetry is consistent with the
  converter story but does not exclude time-value or tick-floor effects; no regression
  on time-to-resolution, number of outcomes or depth is given.
- **Active-outcome set.** Placeholders and "other" outcomes matter for both bounds; how
  the active set was fixed for the 2026 L2 panel is not stated.
- **Profit attribution.** 5-block input and 150-block output windows, imputed spreads,
  and residual mark-to-market drive most of the 1.086M (only 18.9% is full-set core).
  Dropping the 382k cluster is a judgement call (≈ 35% of the total if kept).
- **Sampling.** pmxt L2 hours are a convenience sample from April–May 2026; the number of
  hours and events per hour is missing.
- **Trust:**
  - same-condition YES/NO "arbitrage" is an artifact: **high** (308M-message check);
  - converter ≫ settlement realized profit (order of magnitude): **medium** (attribution
    windows and imputation);
  - YES-side violations far more common than NO-side in CLOB: **medium** (large gap, but
    unreconciled counts and confounds);
  - NO-side episodes shorter: **low** (n = 5);
  - declining per-conversion profit and maker-assisted dominance: **medium**
    (descriptive, partly truncated);
  - reverse adapter adds ~63k gas: **high** (deterministic benchmark), deployability
    **medium** (active-set requirement).

## Directions it opens

- A complete conversion ledger from on-chain `PositionsConverted` (NegRisk Adapter) +
  splits/merges + fills, across both exchange eras: who converts, input sources,
  holding times, and fee paid.
- A live NegRisk coherence monitor from the WebSocket: Σ YES bids − 1 and 1 − Σ YES asks,
  depth-walked and fee-adjusted, with episode half-lives; decompose YES-side gaps into
  time-to-resolution discount vs genuine mispricing.
- Test I1 properly: regress edge incidence on days-to-resolution, outcome count, depth
  and placeholder share; compare events before and after the Adapter (Nov 2023) inside
  the CLOB era.
- Related-party "value transfer" detection: short bursts of conversions with extreme
  return-on-cost against thin books, linked by funding graphs.
- Reconcile the $0.29M vs $39.6M arbitrage estimates on one fill table with explicit
  estimand flags (atomic, converter-linked, settlement-held, inferred rebalancing).
- Maker-vs-taker arbitrageur profiles and the time path of competition (per-conversion
  profit, number of distinct converters per event).
- Product: an "executable overround" API per NegRisk event with depth-to-close and
  expected lock-up, and a reverse-conversion design note for the venue.
- Use the App. A finding as a data-quality rule: drop single-condition YES/NO sum
  violations from any arbitrage scan; treat them as feed-sync checks.
