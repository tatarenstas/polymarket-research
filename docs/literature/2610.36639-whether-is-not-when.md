# Whether Is Not When: Separating Occurrence from Timing in Polymarket Prices

- **arXiv:** [2610.36639](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36639), v1 27 Oct 2026, cs.CY. 11 pp, appendices A–D.
- **Authors:** Sizheng Fan, Yinghe Sun, Rong Zhao (UIBE; Shenzhen Univ. of Information Technology).
- **Read:** full HTML text via a summarising fetch. Not read: the base64 LLM prompt (App. A) and the figure images, for which captions were used.

## Question

Polymarket odds are now cited as early warnings (Dow Jones display deal, Jan 2026; a US
Army intelligence bulletin). Polymarket's "90.4% accuracy one month out" says little,
because 74% of settled Yes/No markets resolve No. The paper asks:
- **(a)** how early prices anticipate geopolitical events that do happen (recall);
- **(b)** how much of the information in prices is *whether* versus *when*;
- **(c)** who earns timing profit, and whether their order flow predicts beyond price;
- **(d)** how best to read near-term warnings.

## Data

**Trades.** All on-chain trades, 2022-11-21 → 2026-09-25, across V1 and V2.
- V1 is the cleaned Qin & Yang (2606.04217) set with true trade direction. V2 was
  "cleaned the same way", but the procedure is not documented.
- 1.24B trades, 2.37M markets, 3.17M accounts, about $110B face value. Metadata comes
  from the Polymarket API.

**Price at time t.** The last trade before t, after dropping trades under $1 and wash
trades. When several trades land in the last block, the median is used.
- The wash filter follows Sirolly et al.: an iterative network score
  `x ← ½(x⁰ + Bx)`, where x⁰ is the share of an account's volume in markets where it
  closed its position and B is the counterparty matrix.
- Self-trades and trades between flagged accounts are removed; 13.8% of shares are
  flagged.

**Event funnel.** 3,686 geopolitical markets → 751 resolved Yes from a single report →
509 events → 296 timed to the hour → **208 dated events in 354 markets**.

**Event time τ.** The earliest web report, found by an LLM agent searching twice, with
a third search when the two disagree.
- The first 200 events were checked by hand.
- The two searches agree within 5 minutes only 47–50% of the time; events whose searches
  differ by more than 6 h were dropped.
- τ reproduces the platform's resolution for 345/351 markets.

**Benchmark.** 51 central-bank decisions (5 banks, May 2024 – Sep 2026).

**Ladders.** 338 deadline ladders (1,551 contracts). 282 of them give 12,573 ladder-days,
of which 3.9% are followed by the event within 7 days. 9.3M trades from 491k accounts.

**Release.** Event dates with URLs, the ladders, and the test inputs. No repository URL is
given, and account-level data is aggregated only.

## Methods

- **Recall:** the share of events whose largest-volume Yes contract is priced ≥ 0.5 at
  fixed leads before τ. A regression explains the price one day before τ by event type,
  log volume, contract age, deadline distance and whether the contract is part of a
  ladder.
- **Event-date distribution:** for each ladder-day (00:00 UTC; open contracts traded in
  the prior 3 days), an isotonic fit of price on deadline gives F_t.
  - Monotonicity violations above 0.02 occur on 0.6% of ladder-days.
  - π_t = F_t(d_K) is "whether", and G_t = F_t/π_t is "when". Values between deadlines
    are interpolated assuming a constant hazard.
- **Exact log-score split:** log₂P = W + T. W is measured against the base rate at a
  similar distance to the deadline; T against a constant hazard, with Weibull as an
  alternative.
  - The event counts as "known" at the price-implied time: the first time Yes reaches
    0.95 and never falls below 0.9 again. That is a median 2 minutes from τ.
- **Exact profit split:** q(y−p) = qG(Y−π) + qY(y−G).
  - Skill tests: 2,000 sign flips per account and ladder; Barras et al. false-discovery
    skill shares.
  - Persistence: earlier half of each ladder versus later half.
  - Group order flow: 24 h net buying by group as a predictor beyond price.
- **Warnings over 7 days:** single contract, π_t, whole ladder F_t(t+h), and gradient
  boosting (features not given). Measured by AUC, AP and precision at a given recall,
  with 1,000 storyline resamples (45 storylines).

## Findings

**Recall at ≥ 0.5.** 56% (109/193) one day before τ, 43% at 7 days, 64% at 6 h and
78% at 1 min. Central banks: 51/51 at one day and 98% at 7 days.

**Median price one day before τ.** Talks and calls 0.97, sanctions 0.76, strikes and
ceasefires 0.32. In the regression, talks are priced +0.37 above strikes, and a deadline
30 days after τ +0.26 above a next-day deadline. Volume, age and ladder membership are
not significant.

**Deadline distance drives the price.**
- A deadline within 1 day after τ has median price 0.10; one ≥ 90 days after has 0.78.
- Within multi-deadline events, the nearest deadline sits at 0.43 and the farthest at 0.72.
- 35 of 180 No-resolving contracts traded at ≥ 0.5 on some day.

**Information content.**
- **Whether:** 0.25 bits per ladder-day, which is −28% log loss [23, 37].
- **When:** 0.04 bits, which is −9% [−7, 26] against a constant hazard and −18% [8, 26]
  against Weibull.
- Timing is about 1/8 of the total. 96% of timing bits come from days with π ≥ 0.5,
  and timing bits are negative when π < 0.2.
- The first deadline priced ≥ 0.5 falls a median 18 days after the event (42/50 events).

**Account skill.** Summed absolute profit: timing $836M, whether $662M.
- ≥ 5 ladders: 7.8% of accounts are significant on timing, an estimated 1.4–3.8% truly
  skilled; on whether, 0.0% are truly skilled.
- ≥ 10 ladders: timing 12.7% significant, 7–10.7% truly skilled.
- Persistence into the later half is 22.5% for timing-skilled accounts versus 9.0% for
  others (whether: 12.7% vs 6.4%).
- Skilled accounts are larger (median volume $120k vs $30k) and are **makers for 62% of
  volume, versus 36%**.
- The top 1% of accounts take 80% of positive timing profit.

**Order flow.** For predicting an event within 3 days, AUC is 0.935 from price alone and
0.923 with net buying added. Adding flow never raises AUC by more than 0.001.

**Warnings over 7 days (AP).**

| Reading | AP | AUC |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Whole ladder | 0.30 | 0.88 |
| Single contract | 0.25 | 0.85 |
| Gradient-boosted model | 0.20 | — |
| π_t | 0.17 | — |

- At a 0.5 threshold, the ladder warns of 18.8% of events and is right 44.5% of the
  time. The single contract warns of 43.5% and is right 24.1% of the time.
- Brier score: 0.033 (ladder) vs 0.062 (single contract).

## Reusable definitions

- Recall versus accuracy.
- Deadline ladder (built by stripping the date phrase) and ladder-day rules.
- The isotonic F_t, split into π and G, with exact log-score and profit splits.
- Price-implied event time (0.95, never below 0.9 after).
- Storyline-cluster resampling.
- Flagged-pair wash filter.

## Stated limitations

τ is precise only to hours. Only 45 storylines. One person may hold many accounts. The
study is descriptive and covers geopolitics only. The authors recommend that platforms
report recall and show whole-ladder near-term probabilities.

## Critical assessment

- **Circular event dating:** sharp price moves triggered extra searches, so prices helped
  set τ. Scoring uses price-implied times, and switching to τ moves the timing gain from
  18% to 23%.
- **Selection on events that happened:** suitable for recall, but recall depends
  mechanically on which contract is chosen.
- **Weak benchmark:** central-bank decisions are scheduled.
- **Inconsistencies:** market counts of 354, 353 and 351; whether-skill of 0.0% at ≥ 5
  ladders but 1.6% at ≥ 10.
- **Undocumented:** the V2 cleaning and the gradient-boosting features.
- **Trust:**
  - recall and the deadline effect: **high**;
  - whether ≫ when: **medium** (direction robust, size depends on the reference);
  - skill shares and persistence: **medium** (an account is not a person);
  - "flow adds nothing": **medium** (only 24 h net buying is tested);
  - ladder beats single contract: **medium** (wide CIs).

## Directions it opens

- Platform-wide ladder reconstruction across all categories, publishing a live F_t,
  hazard and implied-date product.
- A ladder monotonicity-violation monitor, with 0.6% as the baseline.
- A recall-versus-accuracy calibration dashboard by category, horizon and liquidity.
- A V1/V2-consistent cleaned trade set with a wash flag (13.8% here vs Sirolly's 25%).
- Maker-versus-taker timing skill.
- Wallet → entity clustering through funding links and co-trading.
- Automated event-onset detection, validated on the released event list.
- Testing whether order-book depth predicts beyond trades.
- Whether/when scoring for LLM forecasters on the released ladders.
