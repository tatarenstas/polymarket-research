# Can LLMs Help Decentralized Dispute Arbitration? A Case Study of UMA-Resolved Markets on Polymarket

- **arXiv:** [2604.15674](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.15674), v1 17 Apr 2026 (only version), cs.CY. 4 pp
  short paper in an unfilled ACM template (placeholder venue, "2018" copyright, DOI
  XXXXXXX).
- **Authors:** Junhao Wen (Southwest University, Chongqing), Juncen Zhou (University of
  Sydney), Junjie Huang (Southwest University; corresponding).
- **Code/data:** none released. Prompts, model settings, search tools, exemplar IDs and
  the sampled negatives are not provided.
- **Read:** full text (PDF, all 4 pages; arXiv HTML cross-checked). Figure 1 (dispute
  lifecycle) and Figure 2 (category pie) read from captions and embedded labels.

## Question(s)

- **RQ1:** once a Polymarket market has been disputed, can web-enabled LLMs reproduce
  UMA's final resolution using only information available before the vote?
- **RQ2:** can LLMs predict from the rule text alone which markets will be disputed?

The motivation is UMA Optimistic Oracle governance risk (subjectivity, concentrated
voting), illustrated by "Will Zelenskyy wear a suit before July?" (> $200M volume,
resolved No in 2025 after proposals and disputes on both sides). Disputed markets are
said to carry **$972,370,804.71** of volume in total; how this was computed is not given.

## Data

- **Source:** Gamma API `https://gamma-api.polymarket.com/markets`, paginated and pulled
  three times; each pull contained all earlier records, which is read as stability.
  Last pull 2025-10-27 11:58:47 UTC: **140,582** records (called "events" but taken from
  the markets endpoint).
- **Disputed set:** records whose `umaResolutionStatuses` contains `disputed`: **558**.
  The text describes these both as "user-disputed events" and as "individual occurrences
  of the disputed state".
- **Categories:** labels from three LLMs (models not named). 457 (81.90%) fully
  consistent, 98 (17.56%) majority-consistent, 3 (0.54%) inconsistent and assigned by
  hand to Mentions (IDs 542935, 534923, 570937).
  - Distribution: Sports 176 (31.5%), Politics 114 (20.4%), Crypto 93 (16.7%), World 65,
    Elections 34, Culture 34, Tech 19, Mentions 13, Finance 5, Economy 4, Earnings 1
    (sums to 558). The top three make up 68.6%.
- **RQ1 set:** markets with ≥ 1 `disputed` transition **and** a terminal `resolved`
  state: **259**. Example trajectory: `["proposed","disputed","proposed","resolved"]`.
- **RQ2 set:** 9 disputed exemplars + 3 non-disputed exemplars in the prompt. Test set:
  the remaining 549 disputed + 549 randomly sampled non-disputed = **1,098**, balanced.
  Whether the negatives were resolved, and from which period or category, is not stated.

## Methods

**RQ1.**
- Five models: DeepSeek V3.1, Qwen Max, Claude-4.5-Sonnet, GPT-4o,
  gpt-4o-search-preview.
- Each is prompted to search only for information before the UMA decision timestamp and
  to apply the Polymarket rulebook.
- Three independent runs; the majority vote is the answer.
- **Consistency** = agreement of the majority vote with UMA's final outcome.
- **Stability**: very-stable (3/3 identical), stable (2/3), unstable.
- How the time limit on search was enforced is not described.

**RQ2.**
- Structured few-shot prompt using rule text and timestamps only.
- The 9 disputed exemplars span semantic ambiguity, definitional uncertainty,
  evidentiary limits, reporting inconsistency and temporal instability.
- Models: Claude-Sonnet-4.5, DeepSeek-V3.1, Qwen-Max, GPT-4o.
- Metrics: accuracy, precision, recall, F1 against a 0.50 random baseline.

No significance tests, confidence intervals or error analysis.

## Findings

**RQ1: agreement with UMA on 259 disputed-and-resolved markets (Table 1).**

| Model | Consistency | Very stable | Stable | Unstable |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| DeepSeek V3.1 | **232 (89.58%)** | 227 (87.64%) | 32 | 0 |
| Qwen Max | 231 (89.19%) | **249 (96.14%)** | 10 | 0 |
| Claude-4.5-Sonnet | 228 (88.03%) | 248 (95.75%) | 11 | 0 |
| GPT-4o | 203 (78.38%) | 166 (64.09%) | 89 | 4 |
| gpt-4o-search-preview | 185 (71.43%) | 223 (86.10%) | 35 | 1 |

**RQ2: predicting disputes from rule text (Table 2, n = 1,098).**

| Model | Accuracy | Precision | Recall | F1 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Claude-Sonnet-4.5 | 0.5701 | 0.6305 | 0.3388 | 0.4408 |
| DeepSeek-V3.1 | 0.5410 | 0.5680 | 0.3424 | 0.4273 |
| Qwen-Max | 0.4731 | 0.4601 | 0.3157 | 0.3745 |
| GPT-4o | 0.3998 | 0.3618 | 0.2623 | 0.3041 |

- All recalls are < 0.35, so the models rarely flag "disputed".
- GPT-4o and Qwen-Max are below chance; flipping GPT-4o's labels would give ≈ 0.60.
- The authors conclude that rule wording does not decide disputes and that disputes come
  from evolving real-world evidence. This interpretation is not tested.

## Reusable definitions

- `umaResolutionStatuses` (Gamma) as the dispute-trajectory field; "disputed" = contains
  a `disputed` transition; "disputed-and-resolved" = also ends in `resolved`.
- Consistency (majority-of-3 vs UMA outcome) and three-level run stability.
- Balanced dispute-prediction test (disputed vs random non-disputed).

## Stated limitations

None in a dedicated section. The conclusion frames LLMs as complements, not replacements,
and calls for "careful validation before deploying". It suggests future work on the
drivers of disputes in Sports, Politics and Crypto.

## Critical assessment

- **The ground truth may not be a token-holder vote.** On Polymarket's UMA adapter, the
  first dispute usually resets the request for a new proposal; only a second dispute
  goes to the DVM vote. A trajectory like `proposed → disputed → proposed → resolved`
  can therefore end without any vote. The paper treats all 259 as "UMA's decision"
  without separating DVM-voted cases from re-proposed ones.
- **Look-ahead leakage is likely.** The time limit is only an instruction in the prompt.
  Web search returns post-resolution coverage (e.g., the Wired article on the Zelenskyy
  market), and resolutions up to Oct 2025 may be in model training data. No
  leakage audit is done.
- **There is no trivial baseline.** Agreement is compared with 50% "chance". Missing
  comparisons: always-No, the outcome of the last proposal, and the market price just
  before the dispute. Any of these could score close to 89%.
- **Agreement is not correctness.** The 27–74 disagreements per model are not examined,
  and some may be UMA errors.
- **The RQ2 design is weak.** Negatives are random markets (likely many automated
  sports and crypto-price markets, possibly unresolved ones), so this is
  dispute-vs-population and not matched by category. The prompt has 9 positive and 3
  negative exemplars. Recall < 0.35 and below-chance models point to prompt or label
  problems more than to an absence of signal.
- **Units are mixed up** (events vs markets vs dispute occurrences; 140,582 "events" from
  `/markets`). The $972M volume figure has no method.
- **Trust:**
  - top LLMs agree with final outcomes ≈ 88–90% of the time: **low–medium** (leakage, no
    baseline, unclear ground truth);
  - Qwen/Claude stability > 95%: **medium** (same prompt repeated, temperature unknown);
  - rule text does not predict disputes: **low** (weak design; the conclusion
    overreaches);
  - dispute category mix (Sports 31.5%, Politics 20.4%, Crypto 16.7%): **medium** (LLM
    labels, a single snapshot; not normalised by category base rates).

## Directions it opens

- Build a ground-truth dispute dataset from on-chain events, not Gamma strings: UMA OO
  `ProposePrice`, `DisputePrice` and `Settle`, adapter resets, and DVM votes (commit,
  reveal, result). Split first-round resets from DVM-voted cases, with bonds, proposer
  and disputer addresses.
- Dispute base rates by category, template and market creator, normalised by markets
  created, with time from end date to proposal, dispute and settlement.
- Market behaviour around disputes: price, spread and volume from proposal to dispute
  to settlement; does the price before the dispute predict the final outcome better
  than an LLM? Who trades during disputes (wallets, makers vs takers), and their PnL.
- Wallet overlap between proposers, disputers, UMA voters and traders holding positions
  in the market (conflict-of-interest analysis).
- A leakage-controlled LLM resolver benchmark: frozen web snapshots taken at proposal
  time, baselines (always-No, last proposal, market price), and DVM-only evaluation.
- Ex-ante dispute-risk scoring of rule text with matched negatives (same category and
  template), using real resolution-clarification history as features.
- Product: a live "resolution risk" panel (proposal status, dispute flags, bond sizes,
  price reaction) from the APIs and UMA events.
